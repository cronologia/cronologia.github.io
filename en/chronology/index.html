Before 1900

1521
Guadalupe

Fall of Tenochtitlan

After a siege that began in May, the Mexica capital fell to Hernán Cortés and his indigenous allies and Cuauhtémoc was captured; over the following three years the conquerors brought Mesoamerica under Spanish rule and established New Spain. This is the political setting of the decade in which the apparitions are reported, recorded here as context. It is not evidence for or against the apparition account, and no source cited in this dataset connects the two events.

1524
Guadalupe

The first twelve Franciscans land in New Spain

Twelve Franciscans led by Fray Martín de Valencia — later called the 'twelve apostles of Mexico' — landed at San Juan de Ulúa and walked to Mexico City, beginning the organised evangelisation of central New Spain. Their convents, catechisms in indigenous languages and mass baptisms are the missionary setting the apparition narrative presupposes; the narrative itself names none of them.

1528 ?
Guadalupe

Zumárraga arrives as bishop-elect of Mexico and Protector of the Indians

Juan de Zumárraga, presented for the new see of Mexico on 12 December 1527, reached Mexico City at the end of 1528 carrying the title of Protector of the Indians but not yet his bulls: in December 1531 he was still bishop-elect, and he was not consecrated bishop until 27 April 1533, at San Francisco de Valladolid in Spain. This dataset therefore calls him bishop-elect in the 1531 entries. He died in Mexico on 3 June 1548. Recorded as context for who held the office the narrative places Juan Diego before; it is not an argument about whether that meeting happened.

1531 ?
Guadalupe

First reported apparition to Juan Diego

According to the Nican Mopohua narrative, the Virgin Mary appeared to Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin at dawn on the hill of Tepeyac and asked that a shrine be built there; the bishop-elect, Juan de Zumárraga, is said to have received him with reserve. The narrative reports further apparitions over the following days. All dates are Julian-calendar dates internal to the account: no contemporary 1531 document recording the events is known, a silence skeptical scholars emphasize and defenders explain through indigenous oral transmission.

1531 ?
Saints

Guadalupe apparitions reported by Juan Diego

According to the Guadalupan account received by the Church, the Virgin Mary appeared to the indigenous layman Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin between 9 and 12 December 1531, and the image of Our Lady of Guadalupe was left on his tilma. The Vatican's canonization biography relates this account; its sixteenth-century documentation is debated among historians.

1531 ?
Guadalupe

Reported final apparition and the image on the tilma

The narrative reports that Juan Diego gathered roses on Tepeyac at the Virgin's instruction and opened his cloak (tilma) before Bishop-elect Zumárraga, revealing the image of the Virgin now venerated at the basilica. The image's existence and veneration are documented from the mid-16th century; that it originated miraculously on 12 December 1531 is the tradition's claim, first printed in 1648–1649.

1540
Perennialism

Steuco coins 'philosophia perennis'

Agostino Steuco's 'De perenni philosophia' introduces the term — for a continuity thesis inside Christian Platonism, centuries before and apart from the Guénonian school.

1548 ?
Guadalupe

Reported death of Juan Diego

Tradition places Juan Diego's death in 1548, the same year Bishop Zumárraga died. Skeptical scholars note that Zumárraga's surviving papers nowhere mention Juan Diego or the apparitions — one of the primary anchors of the case against historicity; defenders reply that the documentary loss of the period was severe and that the cult's indigenous transmission was oral.

1556
Guadalupe

Bustamante's sermon against the Tepeyac cult; Montúfar's inquiry

The Franciscan provincial Francisco de Bustamante preached against the growing devotion at Tepeyac — days after Archbishop Alonso de Montúfar had preached in its favour — reportedly saying the image had been painted by an Indian. Montúfar opened a formal inquiry (the Información de 1556, rediscovered and published in the 19th century). This is the earliest substantial documentary reference to the image and cult, and it mentions neither Juan Diego nor the apparitions: skeptical scholars, following Edmundo O'Gorman and Stafford Poole, treat 1555–1556 as the real horizon of the devotion; defenders read the controversy as evidence the cult was already strong 25 years after the reported events.

1568 ?
Guadalupe

Bernal Díaz del Castillo reports miracles at the Tepeaquilla chapel

In his Historia verdadera de la conquista de la Nueva España, the conquistador Bernal Díaz del Castillo writes of 'the holy chapel of Our Lady of Guadalupe, which is in Tepeaquilla' and of 'the holy miracles she has done and does every day'. The datable thing here is the REPORT: a named eyewitness of the conquest, writing in the 1560s, attests that miracles were being reported at the shrine. He describes no cure, names no beneficiary, and mentions neither Juan Diego nor any apparition. This dataset records that the claim was made and by whom; it does not record that miracles occurred.

1588
Saints

Sixtus V establishes the Sacred Congregation of Rites

The apostolic constitution Immensa Aeterni Dei created the Sacred Congregation of Rites and entrusted the instruction of causes of canonization to it. That body issued every decree in the Labouré and Soubirous causes below, and its name heads each of them; John Paul II's 1983 constitution on the causes of saints recites its establishment on this date.

1622
Guadalupe

The second shrine at Tepeyac is dedicated

Archbishop Juan Pérez de la Serna consecrated a new sanctuary at Tepeyac, the 'iglesia artesonada', replacing the small early chapel that had served the devotion for roughly ninety years. The building history of the site is one of the few strands of this record documented continuously from the sixteenth century onward, independently of the apparition question.

1648
Guadalupe

Miguel Sánchez publishes Imagen de la Virgen María

The priest Miguel Sánchez published Imagen de la Virgen María, Madre de Dios de Guadalupe — the earliest known printed account of the apparitions, 117 years after the reported events. For skeptical scholars this gap is the central problem of the tradition; for defenders Sánchez merely fixed in print an account transmitted orally and in now-lost manuscripts.

1649
Guadalupe

Laso de la Vega publishes the Huei tlamahuiçoltica, containing the Nican Mopohua

Luis Laso de la Vega, vicar of the Guadalupe chapel, published the Nahuatl tract Huei tlamahuiçoltica, whose core narrative, the Nican Mopohua ('Here it is told'), is widely though not unanimously attributed to the indigenous scholar Antonio Valeriano (c. 1531–1605). Defenders, citing Edmundo O'Gorman among others, date Valeriano's composition to c. 1556; skeptics attribute the work substantially to Laso de la Vega himself. Manuscript copies survive, notably in the New York Public Library's Monumentos Guadalupanos collection.

1666
Guadalupe

Informaciones Jurídicas de 1666

A formal ecclesiastical inquiry (February–March 1666) collected sworn testimonies — including from elderly indigenous witnesses of Cuautitlán who affirmed knowledge of Juan Diego through their parents' generation — to support a petition to Rome for a proper Mass and Office of Guadalupe. Defenders present the Informaciones as juridical evidence of a continuous tradition; skeptics note the testimonies were taken 135 years after the reported events.

1676 ?
Cimbres

An Oratorian mission is founded on the Serra do Ororubá

Fr. João Duarte do Sacramento of the Congregation of the Oratory founds a mission among the Ararobá on the Serra do Ororubá and names it for Nossa Senhora das Montanhas — Our Lady of the Mountains. The Xukuru join the settlement in the 1690s. Two things in this chronology begin here: the Marian dedication of the place, which is older than 1936 by two and a half centuries and belongs to a different devotion; and the Indigenous village that becomes Vila de Cimbres. The 1676 date is the one local historiography gives and is not verified against a primary document here.

1709
Guadalupe

The image is moved to the new shrine — today the Antigua Basílica

The larger sanctuary begun in 1695 to designs by Pedro de Arrieta was dedicated on 27 April 1709 and received the image on 1 May. It housed it for 267 years, until 1976. The colegiata was raised to the rank of basilica in 1904.

1716
Aparecida

The Conselho Ultramarino names Dom Pedro de Almeida governor — the anchor for dating the finding

A consulta of the Conselho Ultramarino names Dom Pedro de Almeida, later Count of Assumar, governor of the captaincy of São Paulo and Minas. His documented journey to take up the post — passing through Guaratinguetá in October 1717 — is the fixed point from which historians date the finding of the image, which the earliest surviving account dates only as '1719, more or less'.

1717 ?
Aparecida

REPORTED: three fishermen net a headless image, then its head, in the Paraíba do Sul

According to the account the Church treats as official, the town council of Guaratinguetá orders fish for the passage of Dom Pedro de Almeida, Count of Assumar, newly appointed governor en route to Minas Gerais. Three fishermen — Domingos Martins Garcia, João Alves and Filipe Pedroso — work a fishless stretch of the Paraíba do Sul until João Alves's drag-net brings up the body of an image of Our Lady of the Conception, without the head; a later cast brings up the head, and abundant fishing follows. Recorded here as a REPORTED event: the finding of a man-made statue, not an apparition — nothing in this case ever called for a discernment judgment, and no Church authority has ever issued one.

1732 ?
Aparecida

The image passes from the Pedroso household to a family oratory

For its first years the image stays in the home of Filipe Pedroso and his family, where neighbours gather to pray before it; around 1732 it is moved to a small family oratory at Porto de Itaguaçu as the devotion outgrows the house. The reported graces and growing pilgrimage of this period are the tradition's own account, first written down in 1757.

1737
Guadalupe

Mexico City swears Our Lady of Guadalupe as its patroness during the matlazáhuatl epidemic

The matlazáhuatl epidemic reached Mexico City late in 1736 and killed tens of thousands there over the following year. As it receded, the city swore the Guadalupan patronage before the archbishop-viceroy: Rodríguez Navarijo's diary records the act in a single line on 27 April. That the Virgin ended the epidemic is a claim, and this dataset attributes it: it is the argument of Cayetano de Cabrera y Quintero's Escudo de armas de México (1746), a work written to make exactly that case, and the same diarist who records the oath gives the intervention no such weight. Britannica notes the same credit as something Guadalupe 'was credited with'.

1743 ?
Aparecida

The vicar José Alves Vilela reports the cult to episcopal authority (reported; attribution tangled)

The vicar of Guaratinguetá, Pe. José Alves Vilela, is reported to have informed episcopal authority of the devotion and the graces attributed to the image, in connection with authorizing the cult and building a proper chapel. The year, the content and even the authorship of the earliest written reports differ across the secondary literature; the dataset records the report as part of the chapel's paper trail without treating any pre-1757 text as verified.

1745
Aparecida

The first chapel is blessed on the Morro dos Coqueiros

The chapel built by the vicar José Alves Vilela on the Morro dos Coqueiros, on land donated by Margarida Nunes Rangel, is blessed and the image is transferred to it. This is the first public church of the devotion; the town of Aparecida later grows around the site.

1746
Guadalupe

The patronage is accepted across the territories of New Spain

The patronage sworn by Mexico City in 1737 was accepted by all the territories of New Spain in 1746 — the step that made the subsequent Roman confirmation possible. Benedict XIV approved the patronage and granted the proper Mass and Office in 1754.

1754 ?
Guadalupe

Benedict XIV declares the Guadalupan patronage of New Spain

Pope Benedict XIV declared Our Lady of Guadalupe patroness of New Spain and approved a proper Mass and Office for the feast of 12 December — the first major papal act in the Guadalupe record and the start of the Church-judgment spine this chronology follows.

1757
Aparecida

Earliest surviving account: the 'Notícia' in the Livro do Tombo — forty years after the event

The vicar Pe. João de Morais e Aguiar writes the 'Notícia da Aparição da Imagem da Senhora' in the Primeiro Livro do Tombo of the parish of Santo Antônio de Guaratinguetá (cited variously at f. 77v and at ff. 98–99) — the earliest surviving continuous account of the finding, written some forty years after it. It opens 'No ano de 1719, pouco mais ou menos', and it is the first surviving document to name the three fishermen. Everything earlier is reported through later tellings; everything later depends on this text.

1762
Cimbres

Cimbres is raised to a vila

The settlement is elevated to the status of vila under the name Cimbres, the sixth in Pernambuco, with a term covering territory now divided among more than twenty municipalities. Pesqueira, in the valley below, is made a vila on 13 May 1836 and takes the municipal seat from Cimbres; from 1913 the municipality is definitively named Pesqueira and Cimbres is one of its districts. By 1936 Cimbres is a village that had once been the head of the region and no longer was.

1806
Medalha Milagrosa

Birth of Catherine Labouré

Catherine Labouré is born into a farming family in Burgundy, ninth of eleven children.

1810
Guadalupe

Guadalupe becomes an insurgent emblem

Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla promoted Our Lady of Guadalupe as patroness of the revolt he led against Spanish rule in 1810; a century later Emiliano Zapata's peasant rebels carried her banner into Mexico City in 1914. The image's career as a national and political symbol runs alongside its devotional one and is part of what historians analyse in the 1895 coronation.

1826
La Salette

Philibert de Bruillard is consecrated Bishop of Grenoble

Philibert de Bruillard, born at Dijon in 1765, ordained in 1789 and a clandestine priest through the Revolution, was consecrated Bishop of Grenoble by Bishop Frayssinous on 6 August 1826. Over his episcopate he established 107 new parishes and 34 vicariates and brought several congregations into the diocese, among them the Oblates of Mary Immaculate in 1834. He was therefore about eighty years old, and twenty years into rebuilding a post-Revolutionary diocese, when the two children of Corps made their report in September 1846. He resigned in 1852 and died on 15 December 1860; his heart is kept at the shrine. Recorded as the institutional setting of the judgment of 1851, not as a reason to accept or reject it.

1830 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Catherine Labouré enters the seminary of the Daughters of Charity at the rue du Bac

Having begun her postulancy at Châtillon-sur-Seine in January 1830, Catherine arrives about three months later at the seminary (novitiate) of the motherhouse, 140 rue du Bac, Paris — a house the Daughters of Charity had occupied since 1813 and whose chapel had been consecrated on 6 August 1815, the company itself having been suppressed in the Revolution and re-established under the Empire. She was one of several hundred sisters in the novitiate; the English Wikipedia dates her entry 21 April 1830, four days before the translation of St Vincent de Paul's relics.

1830
Medalha Milagrosa

The relics of St Vincent de Paul are translated to the rue de Sèvres

A great procession carried the relics of St Vincent de Paul from Notre-Dame to the church of the Congregation of the Mission on the rue de Sèvres, with the archbishop, royal authorities, religious communities and the poor of Paris; Charles X attended. Vincent's tomb had been desecrated in the Revolution and his body hidden, and both Vincentian bodies — the Congregation of the Mission and the Daughters of Charity — had been suppressed and re-established: the translation is the moment the family's public standing was restored, four days after Catherine Labouré entered the novitiate down the road. Catherine is reported to have described a vision of the saint's heart in these days; that report reaches us through her confessor and her own later writings, and is recorded here as a report and not as an occurrence.

1830
Medalha Milagrosa

First reported apparition (night of 18–19 July 1830)

Catherine Labouré later recounted being awakened at about 11:30 p.m. and led to the chapel, where she reported a roughly two-hour conversation with the Virgin Mary, including warnings of coming calamities in France. The account is known through what she told her confessor Jean-Marie Aladel and through her own later written accounts; she remained anonymous as the seer during her lifetime.

1830
Saints

Rue du Bac apparitions reported by Catherine Labouré

Catherine Labouré, a novice of the Daughters of Charity, reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary in the mother-house chapel: on the night of 18–19 July 1830 and again on 27 November 1830, when she reported the vision from which the Miraculous Medal was struck in 1832.

1830
Medalha Milagrosa

The July Revolution — and the archbishop's palace is sacked

Charles X's restrictive ordinances of 26 July brought Paris into the street: three days of fighting from 27 to 29 July ended the Bourbon restoration and put Louis-Philippe on the throne. On 29 July the crowd sacked the archbishop's palace beside Notre-Dame. This is eight days after the first reported apparition and four months before the medal vision, and it is the political weather of both. It is recorded as the setting, not as a cause and not as a corroboration; no source cited here connects the revolution to what Catherine Labouré reported.

1830
Medalha Milagrosa

Second reported apparition — the medal vision

During the novices' evening prayer at about 5:30 p.m., Catherine reported seeing the Virgin standing on a globe, rays streaming from her hands, framed by the invocation 'O Mary conceived without sin, pray for us who have recourse to you', then a reverse design with the letter M, a cross, and two hearts — with the request, as she reported it: 'Have a medal struck on this model; those who wear it will receive great graces.'

1830 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Third reported apparition (December 1830)

Catherine reported a final apparition in December 1830, repeating the medal vision. The exact day was not recorded — only the month is known, as the French Wikipedia synthesis of the published accounts notes.

1831 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Catherine Labouré takes her vows and disappears into forty years of obscurity

Two months after the last reported apparition, Catherine took her vows and was sent to the Hospice d'Enghien at Reuilly, on the other side of Paris, to serve the elderly poor — where she stayed for the rest of her life. She never appeared as the seer: her confessor Aladel refused even the archbishop's request to interview her for the 1836 inquiry, and the pamphlet that made the medal famous named her only as an anonymous nun. The anonymity is not incidental to this record. It is the reason every descriptive statement about 1830 passes through Aladel or through her own writings made years later at a confessor's order.

1831
Medalha Milagrosa

Notre-Dame and the archbishopric are devastated in the February riots

A memorial mass for the assassinated duc de Berry at Saint-Germain-l'Auxerrois on 14 February gave the pretext for two days of anticlerical rioting in which Notre-Dame and the adjoining archbishop's palace were wrecked — worse, Limouzin-Lamothe judges, than the sack of the previous July. Archbishop Hyacinthe-Louis de Quélen lost his residence. He is the authority who, the following year, authorized the striking of the medal: the act came from a hierarchy that had just been driven out of its own buildings, which is context for reading it and not a judgment on it.

1832
Medalha Milagrosa

Cholera reaches Paris

The epidemic ran from 26 March to 30 September 1832 and killed more than 18,400 people in a city of some 786,000 — 12,733 of them in April alone, and 814 on the worst single day. The Daughters of Charity nursed through it, and it is into this that the first medals were distributed in June. The coincidence is part of why the medal spread as fast as it did; it is not evidence about the medal, and the entries keep the two apart.

1832
Medalha Milagrosa

First medals struck and distributed amid the cholera epidemic

After Fr Aladel obtained the authorization of Archbishop Hyacinthe-Louis de Quélen, the goldsmith Adrien Vachette strikes the first medals; the Daughters of Charity begin distributing them in June 1832, as a cholera epidemic kills over 20,000 in Paris. Details differ across sources: the chapel's site says 2,000 first medals distributed in June; the 1911 Catholic Encyclopedia dates the first striking 30 June 1832; Polistena's scholarly study records an order of 20,000 medals placed with Vachette in May 1832, delayed by the epidemic.

1834 ?
Aparecida

Construction of a larger church — the future Basílica Velha — begins

Work begins on a larger church on the Morro dos Coqueiros to replace the 1745 chapel, as the pilgrimage outgrows it. The building, inaugurated in 1888, is the church later titled minor basilica in 1908 and known today as the Basílica Velha.

1834 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Aladel prints the first collection of the graces reported after the medal

Jean-Marie Aladel CM published, over the signature M***, the Notice historique sur l'origine et les effets de la nouvelle médaille — the pamphlet that put the account of the medal's origin and the cures and conversions attributed to it into print, without naming the nun who reported the vision. Ten thousand copies in August 1834, gone in under two months, and four editions by 1835. What is dated here is the PUBLICATION: a partisan of the devotion, who was also the seer's confessor, collecting and circulating claims. That such claims were being made and printed, by whom and when, is the record. Whether any of them happened is not something this dataset says, and none of them carries a Church judgment of its own.

1835 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Mass diffusion — the people of Paris name the medal 'miraculous'

Reported cures and conversions drive explosive demand: the chapel's account counts over 500,000 medals by autumn 1834 and over a million worldwide in 1835; the French Wikipedia, citing the historian Yves Chiron (2007), gives over 500,000 distributed by February 1834, more than ten million struck within seven years, and a thousand million passed by 1876; Polistena records some twenty million made in 1837 alone by Vachette and other Paris and Lyon goldsmiths. Every figure here belongs to whoever published it and none is adopted as this dataset's own. The popular renaming is recorded by the chapel's site: 'the people of Paris call the medal miraculous' — a popular attribution, not a canonical title, and one the French Wikipedia glosses as pointing at the medal's reported ORIGIN in a vision rather than at any power in the object.

1836 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Canonical inquiry of the archdiocese of Paris opens

Archbishop de Quélen orders a diocesan investigation, directed by Vicar-General Quentin as promoter, opening (per the Vincentian-derived catechism used here) on 16 February 1836 and running through some nineteen sittings into July. It examined the seer's credibility (through Fr Aladel — Catherine refused to appear and stayed anonymous), the doctrinal content of the reported vision, the medal's extraordinary circulation (with the engraver's testimony), and attested cures and conversions. Its limits are contested between sources: devotional accounts (e.g. the Association of the Miraculous Medal) state the inquiry declared the medal of supernatural origin, while the detailed catechism account says the findings were favorable but the Archbishop pronounced no formal judgment and proceedings lapsed at his death in 1839; the French Wikipedia synthesis likewise notes no completed canonical trial ever declared the apparitions authentic.

1842
Medalha Milagrosa

Reported conversion of Alphonse Ratisbonne at Sant'Andrea delle Fratte

Alphonse Ratisbonne, a French Jew who had accepted to wear a Miraculous Medal, reported a vision of the Virgin 'as on the medal' in the church of Sant'Andrea delle Fratte in Rome, followed by his immediate conversion; he was baptized on 31 January 1842. A distinct reported event from the Paris apparitions, with its own Church inquiry (see June 1842).

1842
Medalha Milagrosa

Roman canonical inquiry rules on the Ratisbonne conversion

After a canonical process before the tribunal of the Cardinal Vicar, Cardinal Costantino Patrizi, Vicar-General of Gregory XVI, issues a decree declaring Ratisbonne's instantaneous conversion 'a true and outstanding miracle' wrought by God through the intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and permitting publication of the acts — a formal Church judgment on this Roman event, separate from any ruling on the Paris apparitions themselves.

1846
La Salette

The subsistence crisis of the Hungry Forties reaches its worst year

Potato blight arrived on the continent in the summer of 1845 and spread within weeks; the cereal harvests failed as well, and food prices reached their peak in the first half of 1847. France recorded roughly ten thousand famine-related deaths — far fewer than Ireland, and still a subsistence crisis of a kind Western Europe would not see again. Historians read the hardship as the main trigger of the political upheaval of 1848. In the Isère, per the French Wikipedia account, cereal production fell sharply and the price of bread doubled. The public message the children reported speaks of spoilt wheat and rotting potatoes; readers have drawn conclusions from that in both directions, and this dataset draws none. The crisis is recorded here as the material condition of the year, not as an explanation of the report and not as corroboration of it.

1846
La Salette

Two children report an apparition of a weeping "Beautiful Lady"

Mélanie Calvat (14) and Maximin Giraud (11), shepherd children from Corps, report that at about 3 p.m., on the mountain pasture above La Salette-Fallavaux, a weeping woman surrounded by light spoke to them — in French and then in the local Occitan dialect — delivering a public message centred on blasphemy, Sunday observance and conversion, and entrusting each child with a personal secret. All content of the event rests on the children's own accounts.

1846 ?
La Salette

A spring is reported flowing at the apparition site

Two days after the reported apparition, inhabitants of La Salette are said to have gone up to the pasture and to have found clear water flowing abundantly at the spot where the children placed the weeping woman. The spring became the centre of the pilgrimage and of the cures later claimed there. The datable thing recorded here is the REPORT — who said what, and when it began to circulate. This dataset does not record that a spring appeared, nor that it had not been there before.

1846 ?
La Salette

The first cure is reported and circulated as a "declared miracle"

A woman who for twenty-two years had walked only on crutches, and had been bedridden for seven, is reported to have walked unaided after prayers were made for her at the site. It is the first of the claims that later brought the bishop to open a formal inquiry. The event dated here is the REPORTING, not a cure: this dataset does not record that anyone was healed. The French Wikipedia article notes of these "declarations of miracles" that the Church subjected them to no scientific inquiry — La Salette has no institution equivalent to the Lourdes Medical Bureau, and no cure connected with the shrine carries a separate act of recognition.

1847 ?
La Salette

Bishop de Bruillard opens the canonical investigation

Bishop Philibert de Bruillard of Grenoble orders an investigation of the children's report and appoints a commission of clergy and theologians to examine it judicially. What brought it to this point, on the French Wikipedia's account, was the reported cures and the press controversy around them, not the children's account alone: the commission interrogated the visionaries, the inhabitants and the local clergy, and also examined the cures being reported. The commission concludes, per the Catholic Encyclopedia, that the reality of the apparition should be admitted.

1847
La Salette

First anniversary: the congregation counts at least 30,000 on the mountain

The Missionaries' own timeline records "at least 30,000 pilgrims" going up the mountain on the first anniversary, and notes that by 1849 some 15,000 people had been inscribed in the Confraternity of Our Lady of La Salette, Reconciler. Both figures are the congregation's, published by the congregation, and are attributed to it here rather than asserted; no independent count for those years was reachable.

1847 ?
La Salette

The episcopal commission votes in favour of the apparition

The commission of examiners appointed by Bishop de Bruillard votes, on the French Wikipedia's account, in favour of recognising the apparition — with a single dissenting voice. The vote is advice to the bishop, not a judgment: the diocesan judgment itself would not come for another four years, on 19 September 1851.

1848 ?
La Salette

Canon Rousselot publishes his report to the bishop — the printed record of the reported cures

Pierre-Joseph Rousselot (1785–1865), professor of dogmatic theology at Grenoble, member of the commission and the leading advocate of the apparition's reality, published his report to the bishop as La vérité sur l'événement de La Salette du 19 septembre 1846. It is the document in which the cures claimed at the mountain entered the printed record, gathered and argued by a named ecclesiastic who believed them. That is what is dated here: a report about reported cures, by a partisan of the case, addressed to the authority that would judge it. What the cures were, this dataset does not say — the book itself was not readable from this session, and no cure recorded in it carries a Church act of its own.

1849 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

The chapel is enlarged for the crowds

The motherhouse chapel, consecrated in 1815 and built for a private community of several hundred sisters, was enlarged by the architect Gallois to take the crowds the medal had brought. It is the first physical sign that a private novitiate chapel had become a pilgrimage site.

1851
La Salette

The children's secrets are written down and sent to Pius IX

At the bishop's instance the two seers commit their personal secrets to paper — Maximin's letter is dated Grenoble, 3 July 1851, Mélanie's 6 July 1851 — and the sealed texts are carried to Rome by two envoys of Bishop de Bruillard and delivered to Pope Pius IX in July 1851. Accounts differ on the envoys' identity (canons Gerin and Rousselot, or vicars-general Melin and Rousselot). Neither text was published by the Holy See; Maximin's secret was never published at all.

1851
La Salette

Bishop de Bruillard declares the apparition worthy of belief

Five years to the day after the reported event, Bishop Philibert de Bruillard issues his doctrinal pronouncement: the apparition of the Blessed Virgin to the two shepherds on 19 September 1846 "bears within itself all the characteristics of truth", and the faithful are justified in believing it certain. This is the key diocesan approval document; the Catholic Encyclopedia dates the declaration 16 November 1851, a discrepancy recorded here rather than resolved.

1852
La Salette

Pastoral letter: a sanctuary decreed, the Missionaries of La Salette founded

Bishop de Bruillard publishes a pastoral letter announcing the construction of a sanctuary on the mountain of La Salette and creating a body of diocesan missionaries he names the Missionaries of Our Lady of La Salette, charged with serving the pilgrims and spreading the message the children reported.

1852
La Salette

Cornerstone of the sanctuary church laid on the mountain

The first stone of the church is laid on the apparition site at about 1,800 m altitude, before a large assembly of the faithful; the building was completed in 1865.

1852 ?
La Salette

Pius IX grants the new sanctuary a series of spiritual favours

In a fortnight, eleven months after the Bishop of Grenoble declared the apparition worthy of belief, Rome attaches a run of privileges to the site and to the works growing around it. Ullathorne, writing in 1854, lists them: a rescript of 24 August making the sanctuary's high altar a privileged altar in perpetuity; a rescript of 26 August allowing any priest who comes to La Salette to say a votive Mass of the Blessed Virgin on almost any day; a brief of the same day granting five indulgences to members of the Confraternity of La Salette; a brief of 3 September granting a plenary indulgence once a year to everyone who visits the church; a second brief of that date for those who follow the missioners' missions and retreats; and three briefs of 7 September — two giving the missioners the power to indulgence crosses, medals and beads and to give the Scapular, and one erecting the Confraternity into an Archconfraternity under the title of Our Lady of Reconciliation of La Salette. What these acts favour is the cult, the pilgrimage and the missioners. None of them pronounces on whether the apparition happened, and Ullathorne opens the chapter that lists them by saying so: "The Holy See has not formally pronounced upon the fact of La Salette. Her word, that final and decisive word, has not been uttered."

1852 ?
La Salette

Indult: the anniversary of the apparition may be solemnized across the diocese

At the Bishop of Grenoble's request, Pius IX permits the 19th of September — the anniversary of the apparition, "vel ipso apparitionis die" — to be solemnized each year in all the churches of the diocese, or kept on the following Sunday with a solemn Mass and sung Vespers of the Blessed Virgin; the clergy may instead take the whole office and the Mass of the Patronage of the Blessed Virgin under solemn rite, as a Double of the Second Class. Ullathorne draws the inference his readers would draw and marks its limit in the same breath: the clergy "are not indeed enjoined, but they are permitted" to keep the day. A liturgical permission for one diocese is not a doctrinal judgment, and no doctrinal judgment followed it. Petrocchi's Italian history of 1864 renders the formula as leave “per celebrare con officio solenne la memoria della madre di Dio apparsa in forma umana alla Salette” — the memory of the Mother of God appeared in human form — so the wording he had before him still spoke of the apparition itself.

1854
Lourdes

Pius IX defines the dogma of the Immaculate Conception

The apostolic constitution Ineffabilis Deus defines the Immaculate Conception of Mary as Catholic dogma — four years BEFORE the reported apparitions at Lourdes. The date anchors the significance later attached to the words Bernadette reported on 25 March 1858.

1855 ?
La Salette

Bishop Ginoulhiac confirms his predecessor's judgment

Jacques Ginoulhiac, de Bruillard's successor as Bishop of Grenoble, confirms the 1851 decision, stating — per the sanctuary's account — that "the task of the shepherds is complete; that of the Church begins."

1858
La Salette

The first Missionaries of La Salette take their vows

Fathers Albertin, Archier, Berlioz, Basson, Buisson and Petit took their first vows in the bishop's chapel, six years after the pastoral that created the body of diocesan missionaries. Their perpetual vows came in 1879, the same year Rome approved the Institute. Dates from the congregation's own timeline.

1858
Lourdes

First reported apparition at the Massabielle grotto

Bernadette Soubirous, gathering firewood with her sister and a friend, reports seeing a "Lady" in white in a niche of the Massabielle grotto. She initially calls the figure only "Aquéro" ("that thing" in the local Gascon dialect). First of 18 reported apparitions.

1858
Saints

Lourdes apparitions reported by Bernadette Soubirous

Bernadette Soubirous, aged fourteen, reported the first of eighteen apparitions of the Virgin Mary at the grotto of Massabielle; the series ran from 11 February to 16 July 1858. Bishop Laurence of Tarbes declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1862.

1858
Lourdes

Police commissioner Jacomet interrogates Bernadette

The civil authorities respond with skepticism: police commissioner Jacomet questions Bernadette, who speaks only of "Aquéro" and does not claim to know who the figure is. The interrogations by the civil authorities are among the contemporaneously documented events of 1858.

1858
Lourdes

Discovery of the spring (ninth reported apparition)

Bernadette reports that the Lady told her to "go, drink of the spring and wash yourself there"; scratching the ground inside the grotto, she uncovers the spring that still flows and around which the practice of the Lourdes baths later grew.

1858
Lourdes

Request for processions and a chapel (thirteenth reported apparition)

Bernadette reports the Lady's message: "Go and tell the priests that people are to come here in procession and to build a chapel here." She carries the request to parish priest Dominique Peyramale, who demands to know the Lady's name.

1858
Lourdes

"Que soy era Immaculada Concepciou" (sixteenth reported apparition)

On the feast of the Annunciation, Bernadette reports that, asked her name, the Lady answered in Gascon Occitan: "Que soy era Immaculada Concepciou" — "I am the Immaculate Conception" (spelling as given by the Sanctuary; quoted only as reported). The dogma had been defined by Pius IX in 1854; whether the unlettered Bernadette could have known the formula became a central argument in the subsequent inquiry.

1858 ?
Lourdes

The civil authorities close the grotto

Crowds kept coming to Massabielle after Bernadette reported nothing further from 7 April 1858. By the Sanctuary's own account the authorities prohibited access to the grotto "from 15th June", and "signs, boards, barricades are put up"; shortly afterwards the bishop of Tarbes went as far as forbidding Catholics to go there, lifting his ban about a fortnight later. The barricade was still standing on 16 July, which is why Bernadette reported the last apparition from the far bank of the Gave. French accounts date the reopening to 5 October 1858, on an order of Napoleon III. The closing and the reopening are CIVIL acts by civil authorities — the same register as the Jacomet interrogation, and not an ecclesiastical judgment of any kind.

1858
Lourdes

Eighteenth and final reported apparition

With the grotto boarded up by order of the civil authorities, Bernadette reports seeing the Lady a final time from the far bank of the Gave river, "more beautiful than ever". End of the series of 18 reported apparitions begun on 11 February.

1858
Lourdes

Bishop Laurence opens the episcopal commission of inquiry

Bertrand-Sévère Laurence, Bishop of Tarbes, establishes a commission of inquiry into the reported apparitions and alleged cures — the start of the Church's formal examination, twelve days after the last reported apparition.

1862
Lourdes

Decree of Bishop Laurence recognizing the apparitions

After the four-year inquiry, Bishop Laurence issues the judgment that made Lourdes an approved apparition: "We judge that the Immaculate Mary, Mother of God, really appeared to Bernadette Soubirous, on 11th February 1858 and the following days, eighteen times, in the grotto of Massabielle", and that the apparition "assumes all the characteristics of the truth". THE key approval document; the same decree authorizes the cult and the building of the requested chapel.

1862
Lourdes

The first seven cures recognized — in the approval decree itself

The Sanctuary's published list of recognized cures dates the first seven to 18 January 1862, the date of Bishop Laurence's decree: Catherine Latapie of Loubajac, Louis Bouriette and Blaisette Cazenave of Lourdes, Henri Busquet and Madeleine Rizan of Nay, Justin Bouhort of Lourdes and Marie Moreau of Tartas. The datable event is the bishop's act of recognition; the list and the count are the Sanctuary's, and this site asserts nothing about the cures themselves. That the judgment on the apparitions and the first cure recognitions arrive in one document is exactly why the two are easily run together — they are kept apart here, and recognized cures earn no rung on the approval ladder, which records judgments on the apparitions alone.

1866
Lourdes

The crypt — the first church built at the grotto — opened

The crypt beneath what became the Basilica of the Immaculate Conception opens on 19 May 1866, the first church built in answer to the request Bernadette reported on 2 March 1858 ("go and tell the priests… to build a chapel here"). It holds about 120 people. The Sanctuary keeps 19 May as the crypt's dedication anniversary, alongside 25 March for Saint Pius X, 2 July for the Immaculate Conception and 6 October for the Rosary.

1866 ?
Lourdes

Bernadette leaves Lourdes for the convent of Saint-Gildard, Nevers

Bernadette departs Lourdes and enters the motherhouse of the Sisters of Charity of Nevers, taking the postulant's habit on 29 July 1866. She never returns to Lourdes, living her remaining thirteen years as Sister Marie-Bernard.

1868 ?
Aparecida

Princess Isabel's first visit

Princess Isabel, heiress to the Brazilian throne, visits the shrine for the first time; the devotional tradition associates the visit with her promise of a crown for the image. Her documented donation comes with the second visit, in 1888.

1875
La Salette

Death of Maximin Giraud

Maximin Giraud, who after unsettled years of study and odd employments had returned to his native Corps, dies there aged about 39. His personal secret was never published.

1876
Lourdes

Consecration of the Basilica of the Immaculate Conception; the statue crowned

The upper church begun in 1866 above the crypt, blessed on 15 August 1871, is consecrated in July 1876. The statue of Our Lady of Lourdes — carved by Joseph-Hugues Fabisch and inaugurated at the grotto on 4 April 1864 — is crowned with a golden crown commissioned by Pius IX from the Paris goldsmith Mellerio, under a decree of canonical coronation dated 1 February 1876; the crowning itself was performed at Lourdes on 3 July. That date is worth holding on to: the "decree of approval of the apparitions by Pius IX, 1 February 1876" repeated across the devotional literature is this decree, described as something it is not. A crowning honours an image and confers nothing on the reported apparitions: the diocesan decree of 1862 remains the only act that judged them.

1876
Medalha Milagrosa

Death of Catherine Labouré; her identity as the seer becomes known

Catherine Labouré dies at the Enghien hospice in Reuilly, Paris, where she had served the elderly poor for 46 years. Her identity as the seer of 1830, kept between her and her confessors for most of her life (the French Wikipedia notes the secret began to leak from about 1855, and she spoke to her superior shortly before dying), becomes publicly known around her death.

1876
Saints

Death of Catherine Labouré

Catherine Labouré died at the hospice of Enghien in Paris, where she had served the elderly for over forty years. Her identity as the rue du Bac seer, kept quiet during her life, became generally known only around her death.

1879
La Salette

The sanctuary church is consecrated and made a minor basilica

The church on the apparition site — begun in 1852 and finished after nearly three decades of building at 1,800 m — is officially consecrated and raised to the rank of minor basilica, and at the same ceremony the statue of Our Lady of La Salette receives a canonical coronation granted by Leo XIII. Cardinal Guibert, Archbishop of Paris, presided, according to the French Wikipedia account.

1879
Lourdes

Death of Bernadette Soubirous

Bernadette dies at the convent of Saint-Gildard in Nevers, aged 35, after years of illness. Her feast day was later fixed on 16 April, the date of her death.

1879
Saints

Death of Bernadette Soubirous

Bernadette died aged thirty-five at the convent of Saint-Gildard, mother house of the Sisters of Charity of Nevers, which she had entered in 1866.

1879 ?
La Salette

Rome canonically approves the Missionaries of Our Lady of La Salette

The Institute founded by de Bruillard's pastoral of 1852 as a body of diocesan missionaries was canonically approved by Rome, per the congregation's own timeline, on 18 August 1879. This is a Roman act about a religious institute — its constitutions and its existence — and not a judgment on the apparition, which Rome has never ruled on. The two must not be read into one another.

1879
La Salette

Mélanie publishes her expanded secret at Lecce

Mélanie Calvat publishes at Lecce, as "L'apparition de la très Sainte Vierge sur la montagne de La Salette", a text of her secret far longer than the one sent to Pius IX in 1851, with the imprimatur of Salvatore Luigi Zola, Bishop of Lecce, who had received her in his diocese. The expanded text — including its much-quoted apocalyptic passages — is her own later redaction; its relation to what she wrote in 1851 is precisely what the subsequent controversy disputes.

1880 ?
La Salette

The Holy Office asks that the Lecce booklet be withdrawn

After the Bishop of Troyes denounces the Lecce publication, Cardinal Prospero Caterini of the Holy Office writes — per the accounts cited — that the Holy Office is displeased with the book and wishes copies withdrawn from circulation. This is a disciplinary reaction to the expanded secret, not a revocation of the 1851 approval of the apparition.

1881
Fátima

Diocese of Leiria suppressed

The see erected in 1545 is suppressed on 30 September 1881 in the liberal monarchy's reorganisation of the Portuguese dioceses, and its territory — including the parish of Fátima — passes to the Patriarchate of Lisbon. It is still suppressed in 1917, which is why the reported apparitions fall under no local bishop. The suppression is an act of the monarchy, not of the Republic proclaimed in 1910; the two are often run together.

1881
Knights of Columbus

Fr. McGivney convenes the first meeting at St. Mary's

Fr. Michael J. McGivney, the 29-year-old curate of St. Mary's Church, gathers a group of laymen in the parish basement to plan a Catholic mutual-benefit society — a lay-led organization to unite Catholic men and provide for the families of deceased members, at a time when, as the Order's own history and independent accounts both note, Catholic men were joining secret fraternal societies the Church disapproved of, and industrial work left widows and orphans without support.

1882
Knights of Columbus

The first general meeting under the name "Knights of Columbus"

About sixty men meet at St. Mary's church, presided over by James T. Mullen — "the first meeting of those interested in the Catholic organization known as the Knights of Columbus", a contemporaneous New Haven paper reports. The Order dates the choice of Columbus as patron to this meeting — a name that, in the reading the Order itself gives, claimed both Catholic identity and American patriotism at once, against the nativist argument that Catholics could not be full citizens.

1882
Knights of Columbus

The Connecticut legislature charters the Order

The Knights of Columbus are incorporated by act of the Connecticut General Assembly. The Order marks the date as Founder's Day.

1882
Knights of Columbus

San Salvador Council No. 1 is instituted

The first subordinate council of the Order is instituted at New Haven, per the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia's account of the founding.

1883
Lourdes

Foundation of the Medical Bureau (Bureau des Constatations Médicales)

Dr. Georges-Fernand Dunot de Saint-Maclou establishes the Medical Bureau at the request of Fr. Rémi Sempé, the Sanctuary's first rector, so that no reported cure would circulate without collegiate medical assessment. The Bureau examines claimed cures and can declare them "unexplained in the present state of science"; it does not declare miracles — that act belongs to the diocesan bishop.

1885
Lourdes

Foundation of the Hospitality of Our Lady of Lourdes

The Hospitalité Notre-Dame de Lourdes is founded in 1885: an association of volunteers — hospitaliers — who welcome pilgrims and above all sick and disabled pilgrims, taking turns through the year so that the shrine has a continuous presence of them. It is the institution through which the pilgrimage's characteristic organisation of care took shape, and the Sanctuary distinguishes it from the diocesan "hospitalities of accompaniment" that travel with their own groups.

1888
Aparecida

Princess Isabel donates the gold crown and the blue mantle

On her second visit, Princess Isabel donates a crown of gold and gems and a blue mantle for the image — the crown used in the canonical coronation of 1904 and the iconography carried ever since. The donation comes months after the Lei Áurea and months before the fall of the monarchy.

1888 ?
Aparecida

The new church — the Basílica Velha — is solemnly inaugurated

The larger church begun in 1834 is solemnly inaugurated on the feast of the Immaculate Conception, per Wikipédia-pt. It serves as the devotion's principal church until the new basilica of the twentieth century.

1890
Knights of Columbus

Fr. McGivney dies at 38

Michael J. McGivney, by then pastor of St. Thomas Church in Thomaston, dies two days after his 38th birthday. The same-day obituary in the Waterbury Evening Democrat records an illness dating from the influenza of the previous winter, pneumonia in January, and a general decline thereafter; the McGivney Guild's account adds tuberculosis, an attribution recorded here as the Guild's.

1892 ?
Knights of Columbus

Associate members admitted; 6,000 Knights march for the Columbus quadricentennial

The Order opens membership to non-insurance "associate" members, and six thousand Knights march in New Haven's parade for the four-hundredth anniversary of Columbus's landing — both per the Order's centennial timeline.

1892
La Salette

The Missionaries spread beyond France

Fathers Pajot and Vignon reached Hartford, Connecticut in 1892, beginning the congregation's North American presence; earlier, from 1879, missionaries had served ten years in Norway. Madagascar followed in 1899, Poland in 1901–02 and Brazil in 1902, and by 1934 the congregation was organised in four provinces — France, Poland, Brazil and the United States. The English Wikipedia article states the Missionaries now serve in some twenty-five countries. Dates from the congregation's own timeline; the count of countries is attributed to Wikipedia and is not independently verified here.

1894
Medalha Milagrosa

Leo XIII institutes the feast of the Miraculous Medal (27 November)

After examination of the facts by the Sacred Congregation of Rites, Leo XIII institutes a feast with proper Office and Mass of the Manifestation of the Immaculate Virgin under the title of the Miraculous Medal, celebrated yearly on 27 November — a liturgical act read by commentators as implicit Church approval of the devotion.

1894
Aparecida

Redemptorist missionaries take charge of the shrine

Redemptorists of the Bavarian province, brought to Brazil at the request of the Bishop of São Paulo and led by Pe. Gebardo Wiggermann, arrive at Aparecida and assume custody of the shrine. Their custody — pastoral care, publishing, and later a radio, television and web operation — continues today.

1895 ?
Knights of Columbus

First Vatican acknowledgment: Archbishop Satolli's letter

The Apostolic Delegate to the United States, Archbishop Francesco Satolli, writes in praise of "this splendid Catholic organization" and gives it his blessing — the first acknowledgment of the Order from a representative of the Holy See, per the Order's timeline.

1895
Guadalupe

Canonical coronation of the image

The image was solemnly crowned at Tepeyac by Archbishop Próspero María Alarcón with pontifical authorization granted under Leo XIII, amid nine days of celebrations in which the Mexican hierarchy presented Guadalupe as a foundational symbol of the nation — a reading historian Jorge Traslosheros analyses as the deliberate 'updating' of a national myth.

1895
RCC

Elena Guerra's letters to Leo XIII

Elena Guerra (1835–1914), founder of the Oblate Sisters of the Holy Spirit, writes thirteen letters to Leo XIII between 1895 and 1903 urging a renewed devotion to the Holy Spirit; the encyclopedia record links the appeal to Leo XIII's 1897 encyclical 'Divinum illud munus', which established the novena to the Holy Spirit between Ascension and Pentecost. Movement bodies — Pentecost Today USA, the US national service committee, publishes the letters in English as a Renewal resource — present her as a forerunner of the Renewal; that lineage is the movement's own reading, recorded as such and not asserted here. A further movement claim, that Leo XIII consecrated the twentieth century to the Holy Spirit by intoning 'Veni Creator' on 1 January 1901, is deliberately NOT recorded: it is carried by movement accounts only and no primary act was located.

1896
Gustavo Corção

Birth of Gustavo Corção Braga

Born in Rio de Janeiro, then the Distrito Federal, son of Francisco Braga, an accountant, and Graciete Corção Braga, a schoolteacher who ran the Colégio Corção, where he would later teach as a student.

1897 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

Leo XIII grants a canonical coronation of the statue

Three years after instituting the feast, Leo XIII grants a canonical coronation of the statue "of the Immaculate Conception, known as the Miraculous Medal". A coronation is the highest honour the Holy See pays an image and it is an honour paid to an image: it presupposes the cult it crowns and decides nothing about what Catherine Labouré reported seeing in 1830. It belongs with the feast of 1894 on the approval ladder, among the acts on the devotion.

1897
Knights of Columbus

First council outside the United States

Montreal Council 284 is chartered — the Order's first expansion beyond the United States.

Knights of Columbus

The Fourth Degree is exemplified for the first time

Eleven hundred Knights receive the new Fourth Degree, whose principle is patriotism, in New York City; 750 more follow in Boston that May. It is the Fourth Degree's ceremonial oath that the fabricators of the "bogus oath" would later claim to expose.

1901
Aparecida

The Brazilian episcopate petitions for a canonical coronation

The Brazilian episcopate, in a petition the Santuário's account says was signed by Archbishop Joaquim Arcoverde of Rio de Janeiro, requests from the Holy See the canonical coronation of the image. A coronation is a liturgical honour for a venerated image; the petition is the start of the honour's paper trail, not of any inquiry.

Our Lady of Tears

Birth of Amália Aguirre

Amália Aguirre is born in Riós, province of Ourense, Galicia, Spain. The date is attested in the 2023 archdiocesan decree, which describes her as "nascida em Riós, Província de Ourense – Galícia/Espanha, em 22 de julho de 1901".

1901
Lourdes

Consecration of the Rosary Basilica

The church of the Most Holy Rosary, whose first stone Cardinal Desprez, archbishop of Toulouse, laid on 16 July 1883 for the twenty-fifth anniversary of the last reported apparition, is consecrated on Sunday 6 October 1901 by Cardinal Langénieux acting as Leo XIII's delegate; the pope had signed the apostolic letter 'Parta humano generi' for the occasion on 8 September 1901. Pius XI raised the church to a minor basilica by apostolic brief of 24 September 1926. The Sanctuary's archives hold the minutes of the consecration, signed by the consecrating prelates.

1901
RCC

Protestant precursor: modern Pentecostalism

The Topeka outpouring (1901) and the Azusa Street revival (1906) launch modern Pentecostalism — context for, not part of, the Catholic movement.

1903 ?
Aparecida

The coronation is granted (per the Santuário's account, by the Chapter of St Peter's)

Under Pius X, the canonical coronation of the image is granted — per the Santuário's account, by decree of the Chapter of St Peter's Basilica dated 21 December 1903, received at Aparecida on 4 February 1904. The act authorizes an honour for an image; it makes no finding about the finding.

1904
Aparecida

Canonical coronation of the image

Dom José de Camargo Barros, Bishop of São Paulo, crowns the image with Princess Isabel's 1888 crown, before a crowd the accounts put at some 15,000 faithful and twelve to fourteen bishops (the counts vary). Devotional secondaries assert the presence of President Rodrigues Alves, which the Santuário's main account does not mention, and accounts differ over who presided (the nuncio) versus who crowned (Camargo Barros); the dataset records the differences. A canonical coronation honours a venerated image — it presupposes the devotion and adjudicates nothing.

1904 ?
La Salette

Death of Mélanie Calvat

Mélanie Calvat dies at Altamura in southern Italy, where she had settled after decades of wandering between convents and countries. Sources disagree on the day — 14 December (Wikipedia) or 15 December (Catholic Encyclopedia).

1905 ?
Knights of Columbus

First councils in the Philippines and Mexico

Manila Council 1000 and Mexico City's Guadalupe Council 1050 are chartered, opening the Order's presence in Asia and Latin America, per the Order's timeline.

1905 ?
La Salette

The congregation is expelled from the shrine under the separation laws

Under France's separation of Church and State the religious community was put out of the sanctuary and diocesan priests served it in their place. The Missionaries came back after the First World War and permanently in 1943. New roads in the 1960s made the mountain reachable for coach pilgrimages — and three coach crashes on the Laffrey descent, in 1973, 1975 and 2007, killed ninety-eight people between them.

1907
Lourdes

The feast of 11 February extended to the universal Church

The liturgical memorial of the apparitions is extended in 1907 to the whole Latin Church, universalising the feast with its proper Office and Mass "In apparitione Beatae Mariae Virginis Immaculatae" that Leo XIII had granted to the diocese of Tarbes around 1890–1892. This is the furthest Rome has gone for any reported apparition in these chronologies: not an indulgence, not a crown, but a day the entire rite keeps. It is still a concession of cult — the Church permitting a devotion and then extending it — and not a judgment that the apparitions occurred, which is why the approval ladder files it as an act on a related matter and why Pius XII, writing on Lourdes half a century later, still credits the recognition to the bishop of Tarbes.

1907
Saints

Pius X signs the introduction of Catherine Labouré's cause

The ordinary processes having been completed in the Paris curia, Pius X signed the Commission of Introduction of the Cause by his own hand, opening the Roman phase before the Congregation of Rites. The 1933 beatification letter recites the year and the day (AAS vol. 25, pp. 369–370); the 1946 decree on the canonization miracles dates the introduction to 1907 and names Pius X (AAS vol. 39, p. 37).

1907
Lourdes

The jubilee wave of cure recognitions (a count attributed to the Sanctuary)

Thirty-three of the seventy-two cures on the Sanctuary's published list carry a recognition date between 4 February 1907 and 18 October 1913 — the years around the fiftieth anniversary of the apparitions — and twenty of those fall in 1908 alone, five of them on a single day, 6 June 1908. No recognition is then listed for thirty-three years, until 20 May 1946. What is recorded here is the shape of the Sanctuary's own list of Church acts, attributed to the Sanctuary; nothing is claimed about the cures, and the list gives for each case only the person, the town and the date of recognition — not the declaring bishop, and for most not the date of the cure.

1908
Aparecida

Pius X grants the old church the title of minor basilica — the first in Brazil

The church of Aparecida receives the title of Basílica Menor, the first in Brazil, with the solemn proclamation on site on 5 September 1909. A basilica title is an honour for a building and its worship; like every act in this chronology, it is about an object — here, a church — and not about an event.

1908
Our Lady of Tears

The Diocese of Campinas is erected

Pope Pius X erects the Diocese of Campinas, which is installed on 1 November 1908 under its first bishop, Dom João Batista Corrêa Nery. The see is twenty years old, and on its second bishop, when the reports at the Institute begin; it is raised to a metropolitan archdiocese only in 1958. This is context for the later record rather than part of it: a young diocese with a short institutional memory and no established practice for handling a reported private revelation.

1908
TFP

Birth of Plinio Corrêa de Oliveira

Born into a traditional paulista family; Jesuit schooling (Colégio São Luís), law at the future USP.

1909
Knights of Columbus

The Knights of Peter Claver are founded

Four Josephite priests and three Black Catholic laymen found the Knights of Peter Claver, a separate Black Catholic fraternal order. Why a separate order was needed is told differently by different sources: the National Catholic Register writes that it spread where Knights of Columbus councils had excluded Black men through blackball admission votes, while BlackPast.org attributes the founding to the Josephites' concern about losing Black Catholics to the Elks and Masons, without mentioning the Knights of Columbus; the Claver order's own history page states no rationale. The dataset records the framings without resolving them.

1910s

1910
Fátima

The Republic proclaimed in Portugal

The monarchy falls and the Republic is proclaimed at Lisbon on 5 October 1910. The Provisional Government that takes office is anticlerical in programme, and the Diocese of Leiria-Fátima's own historical sketch describes the years that follow as ones of confiscation and persecution — the diocese's characterisation, recorded here as its own. This is the political setting in which the 1917 reports were received and opposed; the site records it as setting, not as cause.

1910
Cimbres

The Diocese of Floresta is erected

The diocese that will judge the Cimbres reports is erected as the Diocese of Floresta, in the Pernambuco sertão; its see moves to Pesqueira and it is renamed on 2 August 1918. It is a young, poor and thinly staffed diocese: in 1936 it is twenty-six years old, and the priest who conducts the inquiry is the bishop's secretary rather than a canonist or a commission.

1911
Fátima

The Law of Separation of Church and State

The Provisional Government decrees the Lei da Separação do Estado das Igrejas on 20 April 1911, drafted by Afonso Costa as Minister of Justice and Worship and published in the Diário do Governo the next day. It ends the Catholic religion's standing as the religion of the State, withdraws public support from worship, places Church property under state control, removes religious teaching from state schools and subjects public acts of worship to civil authorisation. It is the legal regime under which the administrator of the county of Vila Nova de Ourém acted against the children in August 1917.

1912 ?
Knights of Columbus

A fabricated "Fourth Degree oath" enters a congressional election

During the contest for Pennsylvania's 7th district, a fabricated "oath" attributed to Fourth Degree Knights — pledging to hang, burn and strangle heretics — is circulated against the Democratic candidate Eugene C. Bonniwell, himself a Fourth Degree Knight. The winning Republican, Thomas S. Butler, stated for the record that he "did not believe in its truthfulness" and called the document spurious. Surviving broadsides of the fabrication are headed "Taken from the Congressional Record of February 15" — borrowing the authority of the very record that condemned it.

1912 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Guénon's Sufi affiliation

René Guénon adopts Islam in the Shadhili lineage transmitted through Ivan Aguéli (Abd al-Hadi). The two dates in circulation answer different questions, and the English Wikipedia article carries both: it states he was initiated into Islamic esotericism from as early as 1910, and that he first adopted Islam in 1912. The initiatic attachment and the adoption of Islam are therefore dated separately — c. 1910 for the former, 1912 for the latter — and the initiation itself is dated variously c. 1910–1912 across sources. The date stays flagged pending scholarship that fixes it.

1913
Knights of Columbus

A House committee brands the oath "spurious"

In the contested-election case of Bonniwell v. Butler, the House Committee on Elections No. 1 reports to the full House; the report, printed in the Congressional Record of 15 February 1913, reproduces the fabricated oath as an exhibit and Butler's repudiation of it. The committee's words, read on page 3221 of the Record itself: "This committee can not condemn too strongly the publication of the false and libelous article... which was the spurious Knights of Columbus oath, a copy of which is appended to the paper." This is the primary public debunking act, and the document later distributors would misrepresent as congressional endorsement.

1913 ?
Knights of Columbus

First criminal-libel trial over the oath: Charles A. Swift

Charles A. Swift is tried for criminal libel in the Central District Court of St. John's for publishing and circulating the oath. Per the trial record as reprinted by the Order, Swift admitted the charge and expressed "very deep regret... that I should have been led to believe through false representations that this was the oath of the Knights of Columbus".

1913 ?
Knights of Columbus

Philadelphia arrests: the printer and the distributor

Printer Charles Megonegal and barber Clarence H. Stage are arrested for criminal libel and conspiracy over the bogus oath, on the affidavit of a Knight; a magistrate holds Megonegal in $2,000 bail and Stage in $1,000. At the hearing, per the period Catholic press and the Order's pamphlet, Megonegal said he had bought copies of the oath in bulk from The Menace, the anti-Catholic weekly of Aurora, Missouri, before printing his own.

1913
Saints

Pius X signs the introduction of Bernadette Soubirous' cause

The Congregation of Rites resolved on 5 August 1913 that the Commission of Introduction should be signed if it pleased the pope, and Pius X signed it on the thirteenth of the same month, opening the Roman phase of the cause of the Servant of God Sister Marie-Bernard Soubirous (AAS vol. 5, p. 436). The same decree records her death as 16 April 1879, in her thirty-fifth year.

1914 ?
Knights of Columbus

The Morrisons convicted; the real Fourth Degree obligation enters a court record

A jury — with the town's Methodist minister as foreman, as the secular local press noted — unanimously convicts A. M. and G. E. Morrison, father and son, of criminal libel for charging that a local editor had subscribed to the alleged oath. Supreme Secretary William J. McGinley placed the real Fourth Degree obligation in evidence, in what the press of the day called the first opportunity the Order had been given to put the real obligation on a court record. Sentence: thirty days each.

1914
Knights of Columbus

The Commission on Religious Prejudices is created

The Supreme Council, meeting at St. Paul in August 1914, authorizes up to fifty thousand dollars "to study the causes, investigate conditions and suggest remedies for the religious prejudice that has been manifest through press and rostrum" and directs the appointment of a five-member Commission on Religious Prejudices — the resolution as the Commission's own 1915 report prints it. The Kentucky industrialist Patrick Henry Callahan chairs it from October 1914. The Order's chosen instrument against the slander campaign is study and publicity, not litigation alone.

1914 ?
Knights of Columbus

Megonegal pleads guilty; "evidence was introduced that the 'fake oath' was utterly false"

Before Judge Robert W. Wilson in Quarter Sessions Court, Megonegal pleads guilty and Stage nolo contendere; the prosecution — the district attorney assisted by Owen J. Roberts, the future Supreme Court justice — asked that sentence be suspended, the defendants pleading good faith in having received the "oath" from The Menace. The court record, per the Order's pamphlet, states that evidence was introduced that the fake oath was utterly false.

1915
La Salette

Holy Office decree: the faithful ordered not to discuss the "Secret de la Salette"

The Supreme Sacred Congregation of the [[cdf-ddf|Holy Office]] issues a decree — "Decretum circa vulgo dictum «Secret de la Salette»" — commanding all the faithful, of every country, not to treat or discuss the so-called Secret of La Salette, its diverse forms, or its application to present or future times, under any pretext or form, in books, pamphlets or articles signed or anonymous, under pain of canonical sanctions. The decree states expressly that it does not forbid devotion to the Blessed Virgin invoked as Reconciler of La Salette. It disciplines discussion of the expanded secret; it does not touch the 1851 judgment on the apparition.

1916 ?
Fátima

Three apparitions of an Angel reported (retrospective attestation)

According to Lúcia's later memoirs, an Angel — 'the Angel of Peace', who also called himself 'the Angel of Portugal' — appeared three times to the three children: in spring at the Loca do Cabeço, in summer at the well in the yard of Lúcia's family home, and in autumn again at the Loca do Cabeço, where he gave them Communion. The Sanctuary's own narrative dates these only by season. Note: the Angel apparitions are attested retrospectively, principally in Lúcia's Second and Fourth Memoirs (1937, 1941), not in the 1917 interrogations.

1917 ?
Knights of Columbus

The Commission on Religious Prejudices issues its final report

The Commission publishes its reports for 1915–1917 and winds up its work. The Order's own timeline claims that between 1914 and 1917 the number of anti-Catholic publications dropped from sixty to fewer than five; the statistic is the Order's and has not been independently verified here.

1917 ?
Knights of Columbus

Crane v. State: an appellate court sustains a bogus-oath conviction

The Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals affirms Roy Crane's criminal-libel conviction for publishing and distributing a book containing the alleged oath, holding that libel of an identifiable class — the Fourth Degree Knights of the county — is actionable without naming every member, and that it sufficed to prove the purported oath false without disclosing the true obligation. Numerous witnesses had testified to the falsity of the charges.

1917 ?
Knights of Columbus

The K of C Huts of the First World War

After the United States enters the war, the Order opens recreation and welfare centers — the "K of C Huts" — for troops in training camps and at the front, under the motto "Everybody Welcome, Everything Free", open to soldiers regardless of race or creed. The Order's own 1920 account itemizes the money: its independent drives raised over fourteen million dollars, its quota in the joint United War Work Campaign of 1918 was thirty million (negotiated down from a proposed fifty at the Secretary of War's insistence), and its officers handled war moneys "amounting to nearly forty million dollars" in all — figures that are the Order's own accounting, not an independent audit.

1917
Our Lady of Tears

Maria Villac gathers the association from which the congregation grows

Maria Villac, born in Campinas on 26 February 1894, begins meeting with friends for prayer and then for visiting the poor and the sick on the industrial outskirts of Campinas and São Paulo. The congregation's own history counts 364 members in the association by 1927. Santos (2019) describes the same body as the religious association from which the Institute was formed.

Fátima

First reported apparition

While pasturing sheep at the Cova da Iria, the three children reported seeing 'a Lady dressed in white, more brilliant than the sun', who said she was 'from Heaven' and asked them to return on the 13th of each month for six months.

1917
Saints

Fátima apparitions reported by the three shepherd children

Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto reported six monthly apparitions of the Virgin Mary between 13 May and 13 October 1917. The bishop of Leiria declared the apparitions worthy of belief on 13 October 1930.

1917
Fátima

Second reported apparition

Before a group of about fifty people, the children reported a second apparition; in their account the Lady announced she would soon take Francisco and Jacinta to Heaven and asked for devotion to the Immaculate Heart of Mary.

1917
Fátima

Third reported apparition; the 'Secret'

Before a crowd estimated at 4,000–5,000, the children reported a vision they later described as including hell, and a promise of a miracle in October 'so that all may believe'. The three-part 'Secret' associated with this date is attested retrospectively: its first two parts were written down in Lúcia's Third Memoir (31 August 1941), the third part on 3 January 1944 — a sourcing caveat, not a verdict.

1917
Fátima

August: children detained; apparition reported at Valinhos

On 13 August the administrator of the county of Vila Nova de Ourém took the children into custody, so they were absent from the Cova da Iria, where some 15,000–18,000 people had gathered. The children reported the month's apparition on 19 August at Valinhos, near Aljustrel.

1917
Fátima

Fifth reported apparition

Before a crowd estimated at 20,000–30,000, the children reported that the Lady announced that in October Our Lord, Our Lady of Sorrows and Our Lady of Mount Carmel, and Saint Joseph with the Child Jesus would appear, and that a miracle would take place.

1917
Fátima

Sixth reported apparition; the 'Miracle of the Sun'

Before a crowd the contemporary press estimated at 50,000–70,000, the children reported that the Lady named herself 'the Lady of the Rosary' and asked for a chapel. Numerous onlookers — believers and non-believers — described unusual solar phenomena: in the words of O Século's correspondent Avelino de Almeida, the sun 'danced' ('bailou', 'segundo a típica expressão dos camponeses'). The site records what the witnesses and the press reported; it does not adjudicate the event.

1917
Fátima

O Século publishes Avelino de Almeida's report

The anticlerical Lisbon daily O Século publishes 'Coisas espantosas! Como o sol bailou ao meio-dia em Fátima' by its special correspondent Avelino de Almeida, an eyewitness account of the crowd and the solar phenomena; on 29 October the same paper's magazine, Ilustração Portuguesa, publishes Judah Ruah's photographs of the crowd watching the sun. These press pieces are the canonical contemporary attestation of the event — reproduced in the Sanctuary's critical documentation.

1918
Fátima

Diocese of Leiria restored by the bull 'Quo vehementius'

Benedict XV restores the Diocese of Leiria by the bull 'Quo vehementius' of 17 January 1918, as a suffragan see of the Patriarchate of Lisbon with fifty parishes; Cardinal António Mendes Belo executed it at Lisbon on 4 May 1918. The decision had been taken in Rome before the reported apparitions: Cardinal Pietro Gasparri communicated it to the nuncio on 30 April 1917, thirteen days before 13 May. The restored diocese then went two years without a resident bishop, which is why no diocesan judgment on the 1917 reports was canonically possible before 1922.

1918
Cimbres

The episcopal see is transferred to Pesqueira

The Diocese of Floresta (created 1910) is transferred to Pesqueira, taking in the parishes of Pesqueira and Cimbres among others; the Diocese of Pesqueira is the ecclesiastical authority over all later events in this chronology.

Our Lady of Tears

The Aguirre family emigrates to Brazil

Amália Aguirre emigrates from Spain to Brazil after the end of the First World War and, after a period in Bahia, the family settles in Campinas, São Paulo. Santos (2019) gives the year as 1919 and names her parents as Andres and Emerita Aguirre.

Francisco Marto died at home in Aljustrel, aged ten, a victim of the influenza pandemic; the Shrine of Fátima records his death under 4 April 1919.

Fátima

The Capelinha das Aparições built at the Cova da Iria

A small chapel is built at the Cova da Iria between 28 April and 15 June 1919, on the initiative of local devotees and years before the diocese ruled on the reports. An image of Our Lady of the Rosary, offered by a devotee, was blessed on 13 May 1920 and brought to the chapel about a month later; the first Mass was celebrated there on 13 October 1921.

Jacinta Marto died in a Lisbon hospital, aged nine, after surgery for purulent pleurisy following influenza. Her liturgical memorial is kept on 20 February.

1920
Fátima

First resident bishop of the restored diocese appointed

By the bull 'Commissum humilitati nostrae' of 15 May 1920, José Alves Correia da Silva is appointed to the restored see of Leiria; he is consecrated at Porto Cathedral on 25 July 1920, takes canonical possession by proxy on 4 August and makes his solemn entry into Leiria on 5 August. Knights of Columbus

Benedict XV receives a Knights delegation

Pope Benedict XV receives 235 Knights in private audience after their unveiling of a Lafayette statue in Metz; at his request the Order later opens five youth recreation centers in Rome (1924–1927), per the Order's timeline.

Francisco de Campos Barreto is installed as second bishop of Campinas

Dom Francisco de Campos Barreto takes possession of the see of Campinas, which he governs until his death in 1941. 1921
Perennialism

Guénon's first books

1921
Knights of Columbus

Fr. James E. Coyle, Knights chaplain, is shot dead by a Klansman

Methodist minister Edwin R. Stephenson shoots Fr. James E. Coyle — dean of north Alabama, per the next morning's front page; a charter member of Mobile Council 666 and chaplain of Birmingham Council 635, per the Order — an hour after Coyle officiated at the wedding of Stephenson's daughter to a Puerto Rican Catholic. That Stephenson was a Klansman, that the Klan paid for the defense — which included the future Supreme Court justice Hugo Black — and that the jury acquitted are details recorded per the Order's 2017 institutional history of the conflict.

1921
Guadalupe

A bomb explodes at the altar; the image is reported undamaged

Shortly after 10 a.m. a device concealed in flowers exploded at the altar of the old basilica, wrecking the altar's marble base, toppling candelabra, shattering glass and bending a bronze crucifix. The explosion is documented. That the image and even its protective glass were left intact is what contemporaries reported and what El Universal reported the following day, 15 November 1921; devotional accounts have read the crucifix — kept at the basilica as the 'Cristo del Atentado' — as having shielded it. This dataset records the explosion as an event and the intactness of the image as a report, attributed to those who made it.

1922
Fátima

Bomb attack destroys the Capelinha

In the early hours of 6 March 1922 explosive charges set in the walls of the chapel at the Cova da Iria bring down its roof; a fifth charge, placed at the root of the holm oak, did not go off. The attack is attested by letters of 6 and 9 March 1922 published in the Sanctuary's critical edition (docs. 66–67). Canon Formigão's 1923 account names individuals from Lisbon, Santarém and Vila Nova de Ourém as suspected of it — his accusation, recorded here as his, and not a judicial finding. The chapel was rebuilt and reopened on 13 January 1923.

1922
Fátima

Canonical process opened

By a provision of 3 May 1922, José Alves Correia da Silva, bishop of the restored Diocese of Leiria, orders the canonical process on the events of Fátima and names the commission of inquiry (which included Canon Manuel Nunes Formigão, who had interrogated the children in 1917). The commission delivered its report to the bishop in 1930.

1922
Fátima

'Voz da Fátima' begins monthly publication

The first monthly issue of 'Voz da Fátima' appears at Leiria on 13 October 1922 with the bishop's approval, opening with the text of his provision of 3 May 1922 that named the commission of inquiry. The bulletin becomes the place where cures attributed to Our Lady of Fátima are reported: the canonical commission's 1930 report cites it, numbered issue by numbered issue, for every case it lists.

1922
Knights of Columbus

Oregon passes the Klan-backed Compulsory Education Act

Oregon voters approve an initiative eliminating private schooling for children aged eight to sixteen — a measure campaigned for by the Ku Klux Klan and allied groups, motivated, in the Education Next centennial account's words, by anti-immigrant and anti-Catholic views. The Klan-supported candidate Walter M. Pierce wins the governorship on the same ballot. The law, set to take effect in 1926, would have closed the Catholic schools.

Knights of Columbus

The Klan press keeps the oath in circulation

The Fiery Cross, the Klan's Indianapolis weekly, engages the Knights of Columbus repeatedly across its 1922–1924 run — the digitized archive returns dozens of full-text matches for the Order and the "alleged" oath. Guénon's companion polemic against Spiritism completes the boundary-drawing against the occult milieu.

1923
La Salette

Mélanie's booklet is placed on the Index of Forbidden Books

The [[cdf-ddf|Holy Office]] proscribes and condemns the booklet "L'apparition de la très Sainte Vierge sur la sainte montagne de la Salette" — the 1922 Société Saint-Augustin reprint of the text published by Mélanie — ordering that copies be withdrawn from the hands of the faithful; Pope Pius XI approves the resolution. 1923
Knights of Columbus

Carnegie repels a Klan march — town residents, not the Order

Some ten thousand Klansmen gather outside Carnegie for an initiation at Imperial Wizard Hiram Evans's behest, then march into the town, where residents — largely Irish Catholics — fight them street by street until Klansman Thomas R. Abbott is shot dead; a local Catholic undertaker was charged and never convicted, and Abbott's death became a Klan recruiting theme. Philip Jenkins's scholarly study treats Carnegie as the signal episode of Pennsylvania's nativist eruption. No reliable source ties the riot to the Knights of Columbus as an organization, and this dataset does not.

Saints

Two cures declared miracles in Bernadette Soubirous' beatification cause

On the feast of the Apostles Philip and James, Pius XI with the Congregation of Rites declared both proposed miracles established: an instantaneous and complete recovery from tuberculosis of the acute form with occlusion of the cavity, and a religious sister's recovery from a gastric ulcer, which the decree places at the Servant of God's tomb. Knights of Columbus

Pierce v. Society of Sisters strikes down the Oregon law

The Supreme Court unanimously holds Oregon's Compulsory Education Act unconstitutional: "the child is not the mere creature of the State". The Knights contributed to the challenge — the constitutional scholar Paula Abrams records that the Order offered a minimum of ten thousand dollars, within a litigation supervised by the National Catholic Welfare Council, which pledged one hundred thousand — after Supreme Knight Flaherty called on members to rally in defense of the Catholic schools, per the Order's account.

Pius XI beatifies Bernadette in Rome, an intermediate step of the cause opened in 1908. The beatification concerns Bernadette's own life and virtues — canonization causes judge the person, not the apparition.

1925
Saints

Bernadette Soubirous beatified by Pius XI

Pius XI beatified Bernadette Soubirous. The apostolic letter granting that the Venerable Marie-Bernard Soubirous be called Blessed is dated at St Peter's under the Fisherman's ring on 14 June 1925 (AAS vol. 17, pp. 296–301); the Sanctuary of Lourdes' centenary notice reproduces the declaration.

1925
Knights of Columbus

The Klan begins to collapse — from its own scandals, the historians say

At the height of its power the second Klan starts to destroy itself: a Hamilton County jury convicts D. C. Stephenson, Grand Dragon of Indiana, of the second-degree murder of Madge Oberholtzer, and the documents he releases from prison in 1926–27 — published by the Indianapolis Times, whose anti-Klan crusade wins the 1928 Pulitzer Prize — bring indictments against Governor Ed Jackson and other Klan-allied officials. Historians' membership estimates trace the fall: from a peak of several million in the mid-1920s (estimates range from about two to six million) to roughly 350,000 by 1927 in Linda Gordon's account and about 30,000 by 1930 in Shawn Lay's. Scholars attribute the collapse to leadership corruption — the Stephenson case above all — feuding, scandal, press exposure and fading novelty, not to physical resistance by its opponents: reviewing the one popular book that argues otherwise, the Indiana-Klan historian Allen Safianow judged that the 1924 South Bend confrontation "made little contribution to the Indiana Klan's precipitous decline". This event stands here as context: the Knights of Columbus fought the Klan chiefly in courtrooms and in print, and no scholarly account reached by this dataset credits the Order — or street confrontation — with the Klan's fall.

1925
Perennialism

The Regnabit episode

Regnabit — revue universelle du Sacré-Cœur, founded in June 1921 by Fr. Félix Anizan OMI, carries nineteen Guénon articles between August/September 1925 and May 1927, the symbolist scholar Louis Charbonneau-Lassay having recruited him — the high point of his attempted rapprochement with Catholic milieux. Guénon's 12-page letter of 8 June 1928 to Charbonneau-Lassay closes the episode; Guénon blamed 'the hostility of some neo-scholastic milieus' (his framing), while Zoccatelli's study documents intra-congregational controversies around Anizan's Sacred Heart theology from 1923.

Bishop Barreto tells the press the case is genuine

In an interview published by the São Paulo newspaper Diário Nacional, Bishop Francisco de Campos Barreto reaffirms his conviction that what is happening to Sister Amália is a true stigmatization — "extraordinário, milagroso" — and that she is not ill. Santos reads the statement as assertive and out of step with the canonical norms then in force, which required prudence and silence until the case had been verified by suitable persons appointed by the ecclesiastical authority. A view given to a newspaper is not a canonical judgment, and no such judgment has been located.

1928 ?
Our Lady of Tears

Reported stigmata become public; press controversy

Sister Amália's reported stigmata, said to have begun earlier in 1928, become publicly known and draw heavy press attention. The phenomena are immediately contested: physicians and rationalist commentators attribute them to psychological illness, and Dr. Sousa Ribeiro accuses Bishop Barreto of fabricating the case, while Dr. Arthur de Vasconcelos defends the events as genuine in his 1929 book "Res Non Verba". Media coverage diminishes after late 1928.

Perennialism

'Autorité spirituelle et pouvoir temporel'

Guénon asserts the priority of the sacerdotal over the royal — the doctrinal fault line that would divide him from Evola.

1929 ?
Our Lady of Tears

The Institute's own periodical publishes the account of the cure the devotion's origin story turns on

The devotional tradition traces the whole devotion to a reported cure: a man came to the convent door because his wife had been given up by her doctors, and a sister — unnamed in the original telling — offered her own life for the mother's. Raphael Tonon states that he holds an original of “A Missionária”, the Institute's own periodical, in which Bishop Barreto authorised the sisters to publish the account, with the people involved reduced to initials; he reads from it and dates the episode to 1929, and adds, from later family testimony, that the sick woman was the wife of a brother of Madre Villac. What is recorded here is that the account was PUBLISHED and by whom — not that a cure occurred. No copy of the periodical was located independently, no physician's statement or other contemporary attestation of a recovery was found, and no Church authority has ever recognised a cure in this case.

1929 ?
Our Lady of Tears

The apostolic nuncio visits Campinas

The Holy See's apostolic nuncio to Brazil — named by Santos as "D. Aloisi Mansella", that is Archbishop Benedetto Aloisi Masella, nuncio in Brazil from 1927 to 1946 — spends a week in Campinas following the celebrations and the reported manifestations at the house of the Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado. The São Paulo press covers each step and reports him as pleased with what he sees. No act, judgment or report of the Holy See arising from the visit has been located: the visit establishes Roman attention, not a Roman ruling.

Sister Amália reports an apparition of Jesus, bound ("Jesus Manietado"), in the chapel of the Institute's mother house, with the promise — as devotional literature transmits it — that whatever is asked of him through the tears of his Mother will be lovingly granted.

Our Lady of Tears

Sister Amália reports that the Virgin Mary appeared to her in the convent chapel, presented herself as Our Lady of Tears, and taught her the Chaplet (Coroa/Terço) of Tears, saying — as the devotional accounts transmit it — "this is the rosary of My tears, promised by My Son to our dear Institute". Sister Amália reports a further apparition in which Mary asks for a medal bearing her image and the invocation "Ó Virgem Dolorosíssima, as Vossas Lágrimas derrubaram o império infernal" (O most sorrowful Virgin, your tears have overthrown the infernal empire).

1930
Fátima

Canonical commission's report: seventeen cures reported, not verified here

Canon Manuel Nunes Formigão presents to the commission named in 1922 the report it had commissioned; it is read and discussed on 13 and 14 April 1930, approved unanimously except for its chapter on the European war, and handed to the bishop (Documentação Crítica de Fátima, docs. 120–121). Among the material the report weighs is a chapter 'As curas extraordinárias' listing seventeen cases, each naming the person, the ailment, the physician who signed a certificate, and the numbered issue of 'Voz da Fátima' between 13 May 1924 and 13 December 1929 in which the account was published. What is dated and recorded here is the reporting: that these cures were claimed, attested by named doctors and published on those dates. The report's own step from them to the conclusion that 'the miracles of Fátima constitute by themselves an evident proof of the supernaturality of the apparitions' is Formigão's argument, quoted as his; no diocesan or Roman act has ever declared any of the seventeen miraculous.

1930
Aparecida

Pius XI declares Our Lady Aparecida principal Patroness of Brazil

By papal decree of 16 July 1930, at the request of Cardinal Sebastião Leme — who had floated the title at the September 1929 Marian Congress, per the Santuário's account — Pius XI declares Nossa Senhora da Conceição Aparecida principal Patroness of Brazil, with all liturgical privileges. A patronage decree regulates a nation's devotion; it presupposes the cult and makes no finding about 1717.

1930
Fátima

Pastoral letter 'A Divina Providência': visions declared worthy of belief

In the pastoral letter 'A Divina Providência', Bishop José Alves Correia da Silva declares it right 'to declare worthy of belief the visions of the children at the Cova da Iria … on the 13th of May to October 1917' and 'officially to permit the cult of Our Lady of Fátima' (Documentação Crítica de Fátima, doc. 133). This is the key diocesan approval document: a judgment of credibility permitting devotion, not a dogmatic definition.

1931 ?
Our Lady of Tears

Reported episcopal recognition and imprimatur

Devotional literature states that Bishop Francisco de Campos Barreto recognized the veracity of Sister Amália's stigmata and apparitions and granted the authorizations — including the imprimatur — for publication of her writings and of the chaplet prayers. This dataset records the claim as reported by that literature; the underlying document has not been located, and an authorization to publish prayers is in any case distinct from a judgment that the apparitions were supernatural. An academic source located since — Santos (2019) — dates Barreto's imprimatur for the chaplet and the medal to 8 March 1932 instead, and records no episcopal act of 1931 at all.

The image is taken to Rio de Janeiro by special train for the solemn proclamation of the patronage; Cardinal Leme presides before a crowd put at about one million faithful, 'diante do governo e seus ministros' — before the government and its ministers — per Souza. The ceremony is the civic-religious high-water mark of the patronage, and, like the decree it celebrates, an act about a devotion.

1932
Our Lady of Tears

Imprimatur of Bishop Barreto for the Chaplet and the Medal of Tears

Santos (2019) records an imprimatur signed by Bishop Francisco de Campos Barreto on 8 March 1932 authorising the Terço das Lágrimas and the Medalha das Lágrimas to be disseminated within the diocese. This is the earliest episcopal act on the devotion for which an academic source supplies a date and a granting authority. Its object is the chaplet and the medal — devotional texts and an object — and an imprimatur is a permission to print granted after censorship for faith and morals. Our Lady of Tears

Imprimatur for "Licções de Jesus Victima"

Bishop Barreto grants the imprimatur for Licções de Jesus Victima, the volume of messages attributed to "Jesus Manietado" published by the Institute, and writes its preface. Saints

Two cures declared miracles in Catherine Labouré's beatification cause

Pius XI, with the Congregation of Rites, declared that two miracles were established as worked by God through the intercession of the Venerable Catherine Labouré — a recovery from acute diffuse purulent peritonitis and a boy's recovery from Pott's disease of the cervical spine. The decree names both subjects and their diagnoses (AAS vol. 25, p. 161). What is dated here is the declaration; the dataset does not assert that the cures were miraculous, only that the Congregation judged them so. The decree that it was safe to proceed to beatification followed on 12 March 1933 (p. 217).

1933 ?
Medalha Milagrosa

The body is exhumed, and reported found intact

Fifty-seven years after her burial, Catherine Labouré's coffin was opened at Reuilly before witnesses said to have included representatives of the archdiocese, the Daughters of Charity, the Congregation of the Mission and medical examiners. What those witnesses REPORTED was a body intact and supple. That report is the datable thing, and it is what this entry records; the dataset does not state that the body was incorrupt, which is a claim, nor that it was not. Later in the year the reliquary was placed in the rue du Bac chapel at the foot of the statue of the Virgin of the Globe, where it remains.

Our Lady of Tears

Reported episcopal declaration on the devotion

Cimbres

The Diocese of Pesqueira opens an inquiry

After the reports reach the diocese, Bishop Adalberto Accioli Sobral designates his secretary, Fr. José Kehrle, to investigate the events and draft an opinion (parecer) on the credibility of the accounts, following canonical procedure. Devotional transcriptions date Kehrle's first recorded interrogation of Maria da Luz to 15 August 1936.

1936
Cimbres

Kehrle examines the seers on the mountain

Fr. José Kehrle goes to Sítio Guarda in person and conducts the first on-site examination, having already sent up a written note of questions built on an exorcism formula ('Quem pode mais que Deus?') because, on his own account, he feared a diabolical event. He separates the girls, questions them apart, and puts his questions in German and Latin — languages neither could read — with the answers returned in Portuguese; the accounts give some 123 questions on this day. Kehrle reported seeing nothing himself. This is the method the whole case is argued from, by its defenders and against its doubters alike.

1936
Cimbres

Mass gathering at the site; clergy divided

On a date the seers said the apparition itself had appointed, a crowd gathers at the site — the shrine says more than 5,000 pilgrims, Kehrle's account as narrated in the devotional literature says more than 3,000. Kehrle attends with his brother, Fr. Luiz Kehrle, and with Msgr. Elyseu Duarte Diniz; his diary records the message 'Brazil is saved, communism is not coming any more'. The same day Diniz, after questioning the girls separately, declares he believes none of it, an early sign of the split Kehrle's diary describes among clergy and faithful. Ione Paiva, the tradition's principal narrator, says in a 2025 interview that the word 'communism' enters the record through Kehrle's questions rather than through the reported messages; readers of the political material should weigh that.

1936
Cimbres

The parish priest and the Franciscan question the seers; a doctor is called in

Fr. Manuel Marques, parish priest of Cimbres, and Frei Estevão von Roettger OFM question the seers on the mountain in Latin and German. Marques then tells Artur Teixeira in front of witnesses to take the girls to Pesqueira to be examined by a physician; Dr. Lídio Paraíba examines both and certifies in writing that their physical and mental condition showed no abnormality. The medical certificate is the one piece of non-clerical documentation the 1936 inquiry is reported to have produced, and it has not been located.

1936 ?
Cimbres

A second examination, commissioned by the bishop, reports against the reports

Kehrle's diary records that in October Bishop Sobral sent the Franciscan superior together with Fr. Urbano de Carvalho, rector of the college, to examine the events on the spot. They climbed the mountain with the girls, nothing appeared, they concluded the accounts were untrue and reported so to the bishop. This matters for how the inquiry is described: the diocese did not receive one favourable opinion, it received a favourable one from its secretary and an adverse one from two examiners it sent afterwards. No document from either examination has been located; both are known only through Kehrle's diary as printed in the devotional literature. The month is Kehrle's; no day is given.

1936
Cimbres

Police destroy the enclosure at the spring and detain the seer's father

A police detachment under a sergeant, acting in the name of the regional delegado of Pesqueira, destroys the fence and door built around the spring and places Artur Teixeira — Maria da Luz's father and the owner of the land — under arrest, to be taken to Pesqueira and from there to the chief of police in Recife. He is questioned by the regional delegado, found not to be at fault, and released the same night. This is the detention the diocese's own 2021 pastoral letter recalls; Kehrle's diary supplies the date and the sequence. Who ordered it was disputed at the time: the town said the bishop, and Kehrle was instructed by the bishop to say from the cathedral pulpit that this was a rumour. The order, if written, would be in the papers of the Pesqueira police delegacy or of the Pernambuco chefia de polícia, which this project has not seen.

Cimbres

The last reported apparition at Sítio Guarda — and the accounts diverge on it

The 2016 UNICAP study, following Kehrle's diary, dates the last reported appearance at Sítio Guarda to 26 February 1937, with a written message that ends 'Eu aparecerei em outro monte' ('I shall appear on another mountain'); reports then shift to a place called Alagadiço near Alagoinha, still in the Diocese of Pesqueira, and Kehrle sends questions there through Maria da Luz. The devotional accounts do not agree with each other: the narrated video series dates the end to 26 December 1936, Ione Paiva says February 1937 with about forty apparitions in all, and the shrine counts thirty-nine after the first. A date of 11 February 1937, which circulates in search results, was not found in the text of any source reached. The dataset records the divergence rather than choosing among them.

1937 ?
Cimbres

Kehrle consults Germany, and both investigators are moved out of Pesqueira

Uncertain whether the events were of God, Kehrle writes to Friedrich Ritter von Lama in Germany — the writer and confidant of the stigmatic Therese Neumann — asking him to put the question to her; the reply reaches him in June 1937 and is reported as favourable. He forwards it to the bishop and receives back a letter that answers nothing about the apparitions and everything about authority: those who oppose the bishop 'não poderão contar vitória', for whoever is not with the bishop is not with the Church. Around the same time Kehrle and Frei Estevão are notified of their transfers out of Pesqueira. The bishop's letter is quoted in the devotional edition of Kehrle's papers and is the nearest thing to a written episcopal position on the case located for this period — and it is a private letter about obedience, not a ruling on the reports. The Kehrle–von Lama correspondence is a documentary lead that has not been traced to an archive.

1938
Perennialism

Evola's racial doctrine, and what it did and did not amount to

Between the 1938 racial laws and 1941 the political line of Traditionalism acquires an explicitly racial doctrine, and this chronology records it because the gap between 'Revolt Against the Modern World' (1934) and the 1951 trial would otherwise read as a silence. Per the encyclopedia record, writings by Evola in the late 1930s contributed arguments for Fascist Italy's repression of its Jews, and he encouraged and applauded Mussolini's antisemitic racial laws of 1938; in 'Sintesi di dottrina della razza' (Synthesis of the Doctrine of Race, 1941) he set out his ideas on race and eugenics and introduced the notion of a 'spiritual racism' ranking a race of the spirit above one of the body, a book the same record says was endorsed by Mussolini. Evola also said he had worked for the Sicherheitsdienst, the SS intelligence service — an account given by him, recorded here as his own statement rather than as an independently documented appointment. Two distinctions the sources will not let this entry blur. First, contributing arguments for the persecution, and applauding laws once made, is not the same as drafting them: no source consulted here places Evola among the authors of the 1938 measures, and the stronger claim in circulation that he helped form the racial laws is not carried. Second, none of this is Guénon's line: the doctrinal split recorded at 1929 and 1934 is what separates them, and Guénon's own reception has no equivalent chapter. The characterizations are the cited sources', dated and attributed.

1939
Perennialism

The Betteshanger Summer School — where the organic coinage came from

Lord Northbourne hosts the Betteshanger Summer School and Conference on Bio-Dynamic Farming at his farm in Kent, 1–9 July 1939: a nine-day course for British farmers given by Ehrenfried Pfeiffer, who travelled from Switzerland, supported by the biodynamic practitioners Otto Eckstein and Hans Heinze, with Dr Scott Williamson of the Peckham Experiment as the only British lecturer. Northbourne had gone to Switzerland in January 1939 to arrange the visit, having already worked with Pfeiffer on the Farleigh Experiment in 1938; attendance was capped at forty and by invitation only. War broke out less than eight weeks later, and 'Look to the Land' followed in May 1940. This entry exists to keep the next one honest. John Paull's reconstruction — the source for all of the above — argues that Betteshanger is the missing link between biodynamics and organic farming, that a direct line runs from Rudolf Steiner via Pfeiffer to Northbourne, and that 'Look to the Land' took the Steinerian view of the farm as an organism as its central tenet and made it the nominative motif of 'organic' farming, in an account offered to the Anglophone world secularized and at a distance from its Anthroposophical roots. So the coinage this chronology calls the school's most unexpected export has a documented immediate provenance that is not Traditionalist at all: it is Steiner's anthroposophy, a current the Traditionalist authors treated as precisely the kind of modern pseudo-initiatic movement they wrote against. Northbourne's later Traditionalism and his translations of Guénon and Burckhardt are real and are recorded elsewhere here; what this entry denies is the shortcut that reads 'organic farming' as an application of Traditionalist metaphysics. The characterizations are Paull's, dated and attributed.

Basinha Galindo and enters the postulancy of the Religiosas da Instrução Cristã (Damas da Instrução Cristã) at Recife; the shrine's biography dates the entry to 12 June 1940, the start of the noviciate to 14 January 1941, and the temporary vows at which she took the name Irmã Adélia to 14 January 1943, while Catholic press gives her perpetual profession as 1946. She had asked Kehrle beforehand to impose on her, as a penance, silence about the apparitions — she feared the congregation would not accept a seer — and she kept it for forty-nine years. Her fellow seer Maria da Conceição remained a laywoman. The formation dates rest on the shrine's account alone; the earlier reading of this entry, 'December 1940', came from a different devotional source.

1941
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The order settles in Lausanne

Schuon directs the order from Lausanne for four decades (1941–1980).

1941
Cimbres

Kehrle's favorable report is completed — and no ruling follows

Fr. José Kehrle completes his 'Manuscripto', a diary-report concluding favorably on the reported apparitions; it becomes the principal source for later study. No formal diocesan judgment is documented: the 2023 academic study of the case concludes the cult was legitimated by devotees 'without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia', and describes strategies of silencing by the hierarchy — Kehrle kept his opinion unpublished under obedience. Devotional literature (Heralds of the Gospel) goes further, reporting a campaign of suppression that included police pressure and roughly fifty years of imposed silence on the seers and on Kehrle; that characterization is theirs. The manuscript itself has never been published as such, but two devotional editions now carry it in print: Ione Paiva's book, which reproduces its material alongside Irmã Adélia's own writings, and Ana Lígia Lira's 'O Inquisidor de Cimbres' (2021), which its author states is a full transcription of the diary together with Maria da Luz's letters. Collating those printings against the archive copy is the outstanding task on this case.

1941
Our Lady of Tears

Death of Bishop Francisco de Campos Barreto

Francisco de Campos Barreto, second diocesan bishop of Campinas (installed 14 November 1920) and founder of the Instituto das Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado, dies in office.

1941 ?
Our Lady of Tears

After Barreto's death the devotion is withdrawn from public view

Santos (2019) records that following the death of Bishop Barreto in 1941 the devotion to Our Lady of Tears was moved backstage in Campinas: the chapel where the apparition was reported was closed to public access and reserved to the sisters, the devotional image of the Virgin of Tears was taken down from the cathedral altar and moved to the convent chapel, and the book “Glórias e Poder de Nossa Senhora das Lágrimas” disappeared from the holdings of Catholic libraries. He attributes the silence to the death of the case's chief promoter and to a return of prudence, and names no act of any authority ordering it.

Cimbres

An image of Nossa Senhora das Graças is enthroned in the chapel

Per the shrine's own history, an image of Nossa Senhora das Graças donated by Odete Berardo is enthroned in the small chapel that Arthur Teixeira de Carvalho, Maria da Luz's father, had built near the site. Single devotional source; date unverified elsewhere.

1942
Fátima

Pius XII consecrates the Church and the world to the Immaculate Heart of Mary

In a radio message in Portuguese to the faithful of Portugal, sent for the jubilee solemnities in honour of the Virgin venerated at Fátima, Pius XII entrusts and consecrates to the Immaculate Heart of Mary 'not only the Holy Church … but also the whole world torn by savage discord' (Acta Apostolicae Sedis 34, 1942, pp. 313 ff.). It is an act of devotion made on a Fátima occasion; it contains no judgment on whether the 1917 apparitions were supernatural.

TFP

'Em Defesa da Ação Católica'

Plinio's polemic against 'progressive'/Maritainian currents in Catholic Action, prefaced by nuncio Aloisi Masella — a storm of controversy that begins his estrangement from the paulista curia.

Gustavo Corção

A Descoberta do Outro (Agir); begins writing in the Diário de Notícias

Guénon's summa on modernity as the reign of quantity is published in the Liberation moment.

1945
Perennialism

Huxley's other 'Perennial Philosophy'

Aldous Huxley's bestselling anthology (Harper & Brothers, New York, 1945; Chatto & Windus, London, 1946) popularizes the phrase in a universalist-mystical sense — indifferent to orthodoxy and initiation — that the Guénonians rejected as syncretism. Same words, different lineage.

1945
Gustavo Corção

Joins the anti-Estado Novo Resistência Democrática campaign

Takes part in the Resistência Democrática campaign against the Estado Novo. Fleichman's memoir situates his own first meeting with Corção in that milieu.

1946
Fátima

The image at the Capelinha crowned by a papal legate

Cardinal Benedetto Aloisi Masella, sent as Pius XII's legate by the letter 'Celeberrima sollemnia' of 28 April 1946 (Acta Apostolicae Sedis 38, p. 376), solemnly crowns the image of Our Lady of the Rosary in the Capelinha; the crown had been offered by Portuguese women on 13 October 1942. The act honours an image. It remains the strongest candidate for a Roman act on Fátima and still decides nothing about the visions.

1946
Saints

Two further cures declared miracles in Catherine Labouré's canonization cause

The canonization cause having been resumed in 1936, the Congregation of Rites declared two further miracles established through the intercession of Blessed Catherine Labouré: a recovery from severe cardiac insufficiency with renal insufficiency in June 1933, and a Daughter of Charity's recovery from a severe adhesion syndrome on 21 May 1937. The decree names both women (AAS vol. 38, pp. 443–445). Again the recognition act is the event. The decree that it was safe to proceed to canonization followed on 8 December 1946 (AAS vol. 39, pp. 37–38).

1946
Aparecida

Foundation stone of the new basilica

The foundation stone of a vastly larger new basilica is laid. The Santuário's own history records that the stone was stolen that same night and re-laid in August 1954 — an anecdote resting on the shrine's telling alone.

1947
TFP

Break with the curia

Under the new archbishop Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta, Plinio loses the Catholic Action presidency (1944) and the Legionário direction (1947) — ecclesial ostracism. Sympathetic sources highlight a laudatory 1949 letter signed by Msgr. Montini in Pius XII's name; the document is reproduced only by movement sites, so it is recorded as their claim.

1947

The Plains Indian dimension begins

After reading 'Black Elk Speaks', Schuon initiates contact with Lakota tradition-holders; he and his wife are later adopted into the Sioux family of Chief James Red Cloud (1959) and by Thomas Yellowtail into the Crow (1984).

1947

Born in Campinas, São Paulo

Born Olavo Luiz Pimentel de Carvalho. Obituaries note he had little formal schooling and described himself as self-taught.

1947

Canonization of Catherine Labouré by Pius XII

Pius XII canonizes Catherine Labouré on 27 July 1947; addressing the pilgrims the next day (a speech published by the Holy See), he recalls the missions she attributed to the Virgin and the medal 'that the voice of the people did not hesitate to call miraculous'.

1947
Saints

Catherine Labouré canonized by Pius XII

Pius XII canonized Catherine Labouré in St Peter's Basilica, declaring her a saint and fixing her memory on 31 December, her dies natalis. The next day he received the canonization pilgrims in the Court of Saint Damasus and praised the newly canonized 'saint of silence'.

'The Transcendent Unity of Religions'

Perennialism

Evola tried and acquitted

Arrested as alleged ideologue of the neofascist FAR, Evola declares himself 'superfascista' while denying party fascism, and is acquitted — the trial that fixes his postwar political persona.

1951
Gustavo Corção

Leads the Centro Dom Vital during Alceu Amoroso Lima's absence

With Alceu in Washington from 1951, Corção assumes the leadership of the Centro Dom Vital, imprinting on it — in the DHBB's words — 'uma orientação profundamente conservadora'. On Alceu's return in 1953, the DHBB records, 'as divergências entre ambos deflagraram uma aguda crise interna no centro'. 1952 ?
Knights of Columbus

The Supreme Convention petitions Congress on the Pledge

The Supreme Convention adopts a resolution urging Congress to add "under God" to the national Pledge of Allegiance, sending copies to President Truman, Vice President Barkley and Speaker Rayburn — and renews it the following year to every member of Congress, per Columbia magazine's account.

1952
Our Lady of Tears

Decretum Laudis for the Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado

Pope Pius XII grants the Decretum Laudis recognizing the Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado as a congregation of pontifical right. The recognition concerns the congregation, not the apparition reports.

1952
Saints

Informative processes on Francisco and Jacinta Marto opened at Leiria

José Alves Correia da Silva, bishop of Leiria, opened the informative process on the reputation of holiness and the virtues of Francisco Marto, and Jacinta's alongside it. The diocesan phase closed in 1979 — Jacinta's on 2 June, Francisco's on 1 August. It was the same diocese, and largely the same generation of officials, that had judged the 1917 apparitions worthy of belief in 1930; the two judgments remain distinct in kind.

1953
Our Lady of Tears

Sister Amália moves to Taubaté and founds a new house

Santos (2019) records that in 1953 Sister Amália transferred to Taubaté, in the Paraíba valley, where she founded a new house of the congregation; she remained there until her death. Tonon adds that the move was a removal and that she was forbidden to speak of the apparition, and that the house she founded began as a milk kitchen for nursing mothers and became the Lar Irmã Amália. The transfer is recorded here; the reason for it is his account and is not established.

1953
Fátima

Dedication of the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary

The basilica whose first stone had been blessed on 13 May 1928 is dedicated on 7 October 1953; Pius XII granted it the title of basilica by the brief 'Luce Superna' of 11 November 1954. The remains of the two younger seers were translated into it beforehand — Jacinta's on 1 May 1951, Francisco's in 1952.

1954
Lourdes

The International Medical Committee of Lourdes (CMIL) created

Bishop Pierre-Marie Théas of Tarbes and Lourdes, with Dr François Leuret, president of the Bureau des Constatations, set up a Lourdes Medical Committee in 1947, co-opting doctors and professors from outside the shrine; in 1954 the bishop gave it an international dimension and it became the International Medical Committee of Lourdes. Its task is to judge whether a cure the Medical Bureau has found "unexplained" is indeed unexplained on current medical knowledge, against the Lambertini criteria — complete and lasting, from a serious illness of unfavourable prognosis, and sudden — and then to send its opinion to the bishop concerned. Neither body declares a miracle: that act belongs to the bishop of the cured person. An earlier association, announced on 6 September 1925, had become the international A.M.I.L. in 1928.

1954 ?
Our Lady of Tears

Madre Maria Villac among the founders of the Conference of Religious of Brazil

The congregation records that its foundress, Madre Maria Villac, was one of the founders of the Conferência dos Religiosos do Brasil (CRB) in 1954 and served for several years as a general counsellor — an indication of the standing the Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado had reached nationally two years after the Decretum Laudis, and of a public life for the congregation entirely separate from the apparition reports.

1954
Knights of Columbus

"Under God" enters the national Pledge

On Flag Day, President Eisenhower signs the joint resolution adding "under God" to the Pledge of Allegiance. The Order regards the change as the outcome of the campaign it began in 1951; the legislative history had many actors, and the credit claim is recorded here as the Order's.

1954
Gustavo Corção

His column syndicates across the Brazilian press

The collaboration widens: O Estado de S. Paulo from 1954, Correio do Povo from 1956, Gazeta do Povo and A Tarde from 1960.

1954 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

Childhood illness — his own account

His most repeated origin story in the course: sick from birth and bedridden through his first seven years with constant fever, then suddenly and inexplicably cured around age seven, going straight to school afterward (told, among other places, in the lectures of 9 October 2010 and 20 April 2013). In a lecture of 16 January 2010 he added that he was born with an undiagnosed tracheal tumor still with him in his sixties; in a lecture of c. 2020 he tied his late-diagnosed emphysema to a lung operation decades earlier. All of this is his own retrospective testimony via the community transcriptions, unverified against audio — recorded here as testimony, not as a medical record. A second line of his own testimony describes the condition itself, twice. On 10 August 2009 he said he had been born with a tracheal tumour and an infectious bronchitis that was near-fatal for seven years — though a fortnight later he said instead that nobody had known what he had, a wobble worth recording rather than smoothing. Again on his programme of 14 April 2010 — eight years before the surgery and twelve before his death — he said he had been born with a tracheal tumour, which he sized by comparison to an orange. In the same passage he said he had smoked since fifteen, three packs a day for over forty years. Both are his own statements in machine-transcribed broadcasts and neither is a medical record; the first is the earliest first-person account yet found of the congenital condition that the 2018 surgery would remove, and it long predates the disputes that followed his death.

1955
CELAM

I General Conference

Convoked by Pius XII, the first General Conference of the Latin American Episcopate petitions for a permanent coordinating body.

1955
Liberation Theology

CELAM founded

At the First General Conference of the Latin American Episcopate (Rio de Janeiro, 25 July–4 August 1955), convoked by Pius XII through the apostolic letter 'Ad Ecclesiam Christi', the bishops resolved to create the Latin American Episcopal Council; Pius XII approved its canonical constitution on 2 November 1955, with headquarters in Bogotá.

1955 ?
CELAM

Pius XII approves CELAM

The pope approves the creation of the Consejo Episcopal Latinoamericano; one source records a 24 September approval with the formal founding document on 2 November — the sequence is recorded, not resolved.

1955
Aparecida

Effective construction of the new basilica begins

Construction of the new basilica begins in earnest to the design of Benedito Calixto de Jesus Neto: a Greek cross of 188 × 183 m, a 70 m dome, a 109 m tower, some 23,000 m² of interior and a capacity of 30,000. The building will take decades; pilgrims begin using the unfinished nave in 1959.

1956 ?
CELAM

Secretariat in Bogotá; first Assembly

Bogotá is designated as seat (Rome having been considered and rejected, per DHIAL); the first ordinary General Assembly elects Cardinal Jaime de Barros Câmara (Rio) first president.

1958
Perennialism

'Sacred Art in East and West'

Titus Burckhardt's canonical Traditionalist theory of sacred art across five traditions (English translation by Lord Northbourne).

1958
Perennialism

'The Religions of Man'

Huston Smith's textbook (later 'The World's Religions') dominates American comparative-religion teaching; his openly perennialist 'Forgotten Truth' (1976) follows under Schuon's influence.

1958
Lourdes

Consecration of the underground Basilica of Saint Pius X

The underground basilica of Saint Pius X, built in prestressed concrete to hold crowds the three earlier churches could not, is consecrated on 25 March 1958, the centenary year of the reported apparitions and the anniversary of the day the name "Immaculate Conception" was reported at the grotto. 25 March is the date the Sanctuary keeps as its dedication anniversary.

1958
CELAM

CAL created

Pius XII creates the Pontifical Commission for Latin America in the Roman Curia — CELAM's standing liaison with the Holy See.

1958
Aparecida

Pius XII erects the Archdiocese of Aparecida — directly as an archdiocese

By the apostolic constitution Sacrorum antistitum, Pius XII erects the Archdiocese of Aparecida — directly as a metropolitan archdiocese, not as a diocese later elevated. The see is governed by apostolic administrators until 1964. The erection of a see around a shrine is an act of ecclesiastical organization; it honours the devotion's weight and judges nothing about its origin.

1959
CELAM

CLAR erected

The Holy See erects the Confederación Latinoamericana de Religiosos, CELAM's sister body for religious life.

1959
TFP

'Revolução e Contra-Revolução'

Published in Catolicismo's 100th issue: the movement's doctrinal matrix, reading modern history as a five-century 'Revolution' and calling for a 'Counter-Revolution' — Plinio's technical vocabulary, attributed to the text.

1959
Gustavo Corção

Jacques Maritain writes to Corção

Maritain's letter of 9 August 1959 to Corção — a facsimile is printed in the Biblioteca Nacional's Revista do Livro n.57 dossier. Jalles de Paula dates Corção's later rupture with 'seu mestre de conversão' to the Vatican II years, and O Século do Nada (1973) attacks Maritain directly (the latter point via an admirer's chronology — sympathetic secondary source).

1960s

1960 ?
Knights of Columbus

The oath resurfaces against John F. Kennedy

During the campaign of the second Catholic major-party nominee — himself a Fourth Degree Knight — the fabrication circulates again, through newsletters and sermons; Time recounts its 1912 origin and the 1913 congressional committee's condemnation, notes a clergyman facing criminal-libel charges for refusing to retract it, and prints the genuine obligation. The Order, under Supreme Knight Luke E. Hart, answers again through publications, lawsuits and public statements, per its own account.

1960
TFP

TFP founded

The Sociedade Brasileira de Defesa da Tradição, Família e Propriedade is founded; co-founders include Adolpho Lindenberg, Plinio Vidigal Xavier da Silveira and José Fernando de Camargo — the lists vary slightly between sources.

1960
TFP

'Reforma Agrária: Questão de Consciência'

Four authors — Bishop Geraldo de Proença Sigaud, Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer, Plinio Corrêa de Oliveira and the economist Luiz Mendonça de Freitas — publish the anti-agrarian-reform manifesto; about 30,000 copies over four editions (movement figures, attributed).

1961
Perennialism

Evola's postwar canon

'Men Among the Ruins' (1953) and 'Ride the Tiger' (1961) join 'Revolt' as the canon of the postwar Italian radical right, of which scholarship describes Evola as chief ideologue.

1961
Perennialism

'Understanding Islam'

Schuon's most popular book (French 'Comprendre l'Islam', Gallimard, 1961; English 1963) becomes a key channel of perennialism into Islamic studies. The 1961 first edition is confirmed by the official Schuon bibliography and Gallimard.

1961
Liberation Theology

ISAL founded

Iglesia y Sociedad en América Latina (ISAL) — the radicalizing Protestant 'church and society' network (Rubem Alves, Richard Shaull, José Míguez Bonino) that was a precursor stream to liberation theology — grew out of the First Latin American Consultation on Church and Society, held at Huampaní, Peru, in July 1961 and backed by the World Council of Churches; the consultation created the Junta Latinoamericana de Iglesia y Sociedad. Scholarship places the founding consultation at Huampaní; the occasional 'Huancayo' attribution is unsupported.

1962
La Salette

The Association of the Pilgrims of Notre-Dame de La Salette is created

The sanctuary's own history page records the creation in 1962 of the Association des Pèlerins de Notre-Dame de La Salette, to maintain and administer the buildings and the infrastructure that a mountain shrine at 1,800 m requires. It is the civil body behind the pilgrimage as it now runs.

1962
Liberation Theology

Second Vatican Council opens

The Council (1962–1965) opens the space that Latin American bishops later 'receive' at Medellín.

1963
TFP

The Coetus Internationalis Patrum

Sigaud, Castro Mayer and Marcel Lefebvre articulate the conservative minority of Vatican II; movement sources describe Plinio as the group's lay strategist — that role rests on sympathetic sources and is attributed.

1963
Gustavo Corção

Leaves the Centro Dom Vital with about 200 associates

After Alceu Amoroso Lima's Jornal do Brasil page defending the Church of dialogue — 'uma espécie de Encíclica do Alceu', in Corção's phrase as recalled by Fleichman — Corção, then vice-president of the Centro Dom Vital and editor of A Ordem, leaves the Centro taking about 200 associates. Alceu's contemporaneous reading, quoted by Jalles de Paula: 'o medo da liberdade e o culto da autoridade tornaram-se as bases dos argumentos' of Corção. Fleichman's memoir has Cardinal Jaime Câmara siding with Corção's group ('vocês fiquem, quem vai sair é ele') — Permanência-side testimony, labeled.

1964 ?
Knights of Columbus

McDevitt moves against racial exclusion in admissions

New Supreme Knight John W. McDevitt makes it a first priority to amend admission policies — in the Order's own words, "to counter charges of racial discrimination" — and the blackball veto by which individual councils had excluded Black applicants is subsequently eliminated. The Order's phrasing acknowledges the charges; the exclusions themselves are documented in the founding context of the Knights of Peter Claver (1909).

1964
Gustavo Corção

A Ordem stops circulating; the Centro Dom Vital declines

The Centro Dom Vital's review A Ordem stops circulating 'devido a divergências entre seus colaboradores' (DHBB). Alceu Amoroso Lima leaves the Centro's presidency in 1966; the DHBB records the Centro's decline thereafter.

1964
Liberation Theology

Hélder Câmara named Archbishop of Olinda and Recife

Dom Hélder Câmara was appointed Archbishop of Olinda and Recife on 12 March 1964, weeks before the military coup; from that see he became a leading pastoral voice for the poor and a persistent target of the dictatorship, though he was never a systematic liberation theologian.

1964
Gustavo Corção

Hails the 1964 coup in the Diário de Notícias

In columns of 3 and 5 April 1964, quoted by Jalles de Paula, Corção greets the military movement as the 'triunfo da democracia' and argues against restoring 'os direitos que eles mesmos, violentamente, destruíram'. His summary formula, quoted in the DHBB: the movement 'possibilitou, com um mínimo de choques, libertarmo-nos da sarna esquerdista que já estava no poder'. The characterizations of what this made him are attributed both ways in this dataset — see the disambiguation section.

1964
Aparecida

Cardinal Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta is appointed the first resident Archbishop of Aparecida, ending the apostolic administration that had run since the see's erection in 1958. Some accounts misread 1964 as the see's elevation to archdiocese; it is the first archbishop's appointment — the see was an archdiocese from 1958.

1965 ?
TFP

The street campaigns

Caravans under red standards with a golden lion, public rosaries and signature drives against communism and 'socialist and confiscatory' agrarian reform make the TFP a fixture of Brazilian streets.

1965
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Marian visions

Schuon reports visions of the Virgin Mary ('Marian graces') and thereafter regards Mary as the order's spiritual protectress — the origin of the order's distinctive Marian devotion. Sedgwick (14 July 2010) ties the order's foundation as a distinct tariqa to “the authorization said to have been given to Schuon in a vision by the Virgin Mary”, paralleling it with the authorization “said to have been given to al-Alawi in a vision by Ali ibn Abi Talib” and adding that “while such forms of authorization are not universal, they are very common.” His conclusion is deliberately conditional: “the Maryamiyya is probably ‘valid’ to the extent that Schuon’s vision of the Virgin Mary in 1965 was ‘valid.’” Answering a commenter the same day, he stressed that he had expressed no view on whether the vision was valid.

1965 ?
CELAM

Expansion to nine departments

CELAM grows from two sub-departments to nine specialized departments (education, liturgy, seminaries, social action…).

1965
Liberation Theology

Camilo Torres laicized; leads the Frente Unido

Having co-founded Latin America's first sociology faculty at the Universidad Nacional after studying at Louvain, Torres asked to be reduced to lay status amid conflict with Cardinal Concha and gave his final Mass at the Church of San Diego, devoting himself to the Frente Unido popular-front movement whose weekly newspaper had launched that August. Months later he joined the ELN guerrilla.

1965
Liberation Theology

Pact of the Catacombs

Three weeks before Vatican II closed, some 40 bishops celebrated Mass in the Catacombs of Domitilla and signed the 'Pact of the Catacombs', pledging to live simply and make a 'church of the poor' central to their ministry. Hélder Câmara was a leading promoter; over 500 bishops later added their signatures. The pact became closely associated with the emerging current.

1965
Gustavo Corção

The linha-dura option; campaigns against Alceu, Dom Hélder and Sobral Pinto

Jalles de Paula documents the turn: on the Arraes habeas corpus, 28 October 1965, 'Acima do STF está o espírito democrático da Revolução'; a crônica of 16 September accuses Alceu Amoroso Lima of inciting youth 'pela causa comunista'; campaigns follow against Dom Hélder Câmara and even Sobral Pinto. Jalles's thesis — that Corção was 'um dos artífices' of the legitimation of the regime's anti-democratic bases — is his reading, recorded here as such; the admiring profiles omit this thread entirely, which is recorded too.

1966 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

Brief affiliation with the Brazilian Communist Party (PCB)

A BBC Brasil profile built on an in-person interview (Fellet, December 2016) states 'Entre 1966 e 1968, militou no Partido Comunista'; the historian Gilberto Calil records a brief PCB passage in the 1960s without exact dates. The dating rests on his own recollection as relayed by the interviewer, so the flag stays. In the course he bracketed the period himself: in 1964 he was a secondary student with vague leftist sympathy, not yet a militant, and he recalled joining the party 'to stir up trouble' and spending twenty years working the influence out of himself (lectures of 2009 and 2014, per the community transcriptions).

1966
Liberation Theology

The Colombian priest and sociologist, who had joined the ELN guerrilla, is killed in his first combat; later invoked as a precursor symbol, though his choice of armed struggle was not representative of liberation theologians.

1966

The president dies in a car accident; the X Ordinary Assembly (Mar del Plata 1966) elects Avelar Brandão Vilela to complete the term.

Indiana University professor Victor Danner founds the Bloomington chapter, which becomes the order's most important American center.

1967

Ralph Martin, Stephen Clark, Jim Cavnar and Gerry Rauch found the Word of God — matrix of the 'covenant communities'.

The DHBB has him ending the Diário de Notícias collaboration in 1967 and joining the newly created Conselho Federal de Cultura the same year; Jalles de Paula has him leaving the paper in March 1968.

About 25 students of Duquesne University's Chi Rho group, on retreat at The Ark and the Dove, report experiences of baptism in the Holy Spirit — the founding moment of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal. Some popular accounts say 17–18 February; 17–19 is the solid dating.

Two weeks after Duquesne, nine people — among them Kevin and Dorothy Ranaghan — pray for baptism in the Spirit at the University of Notre Dame; the first 'international' gathering follows there on 7–9 April, seed of the annual Notre Dame conferences that would draw tens of thousands in the 1970s.

Liberation Theology

Populorum Progressio

Paul VI's encyclical on 'the development of peoples', a key reference for the movement.

1967
Fátima

Paul VI's pilgrimage to Fátima

Paul VI comes to the Cova da Iria on 13 May 1967 — the fiftieth anniversary of the first reported apparition and the twenty-fifth of the 1942 consecration — and celebrates Mass in the basilica. In the homily he calls himself a 'humble and trusting pilgrim' and prays for the peace of the Church and of the world. It is the first visit by a reigning pope. It is a visit, and it rules on nothing.

1967 ?
Aparecida

Golden rose no. 1 — Paul VI, for the 250th jubilee of the finding

Paul VI grants the shrine its first golden rose for the 250th anniversary of the finding, delivered — per the Santuário's account — on 15 August 1967. The golden rose is a papal honour bestowed on shrines, images and persons; it decorates a devotion and certifies nothing about its origin.

1968
Gustavo Corção

First crônica in O Globo

'Quinta-feira Santa', his first crônica in O Globo, appears on 11 April 1968; he writes there on Thursdays and Saturdays until 1978.

1968
Liberation Theology

Gutiérrez's Chimbote lecture

Asked to speak on a 'theology of development', Gustavo Gutiérrez reframes the topic as 'Hacia una teología de la liberación'.

1968
TFP

The petition to Paul VI

The 'Message to Paul VI' against 'communist infiltration' in the Church gathers 1,600,368 signatures in Brazil (2,025,201 with the Southern Cone TFPs) — the movement's own numbers, always attributed.

1968
Gustavo Corção

Public launch of Permanência packs the MEC auditorium

Corção's launch conference for the 'associação cultural católica Permanência' fills the auditorium of the Ministry of Education. O Globo of 19 August 1968, cited by Jalles de Paula, reports the objective as 'o combate às confusões e deformações da Igreja no Brasil, introduzidas pela chamada ala progressista do clero'.

1968
Liberation Theology

CELAM II (Medellín)

Paul VI opens the conference in Bogotá on 24 August (first papal visit to Latin America); working sessions in Medellín 26 August – 6 September adopt the language of 'institutionalized violence' and the option for the poor.

1968
CELAM

II General Conference (Medellín)

Paul VI opens the conference in Bogotá cathedral — the first papal visit to Latin America; the working sessions sit in Medellín, producing the Medellín Documents. The interpretation disputes belong to the tl project.

1968
Gustavo Corção

Open letter to the nuncio, Dom Sebastiano Baggio

Corção and his partisans sign an open letter to the apostolic nuncio denouncing what they call the 'deturpada' reception of the Council in Brazil.

1968
Gustavo Corção

Permanência's religious founding, with a mass by Cardinal Jaime Câmara

The date Permanência itself keeps as its founding: 'Fundada em 29 de Setembro de 1968, por Gustavo Corção e diversos intelectuais católicos egressos do Centro Dom Vital', on the feast of St Michael, with a founding mass celebrated by Cardinal Jaime Câmara.

1968
Gustavo Corção

O Cruzeiro reports the revista Permanência's launch

O Cruzeiro of 12 October 1968, cited by Jalles de Paula, reports the revista launched with 1,000 subscribers, Corção hoping for 5,000. The revista was directed by Alfredo Lage.

1969
Liberation Theology

Rubem Alves, 'A Theology of Human Hope'

Alves's Princeton dissertation, defended in 1968 as 'Towards a Theology of Liberation', was published in 1969 as 'A Theology of Human Hope'; his publisher required the retitling for the US market. Harvey Cox later observed that but for the change, Alves rather than Gutiérrez might be remembered as the 'father of liberation theology'.

1969 ?
Liberation Theology

Frei Betto imprisoned under the military dictatorship

The Dominican friar Frei Betto (Carlos Alberto Libânio Christo) was arrested and held from 1969 to 1973 for aiding opponents of Brazil's military regime, passing through several prisons; the experience shaped his 'Cartas da Prisão' and later 'Batismo de Sangue'.

1969
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'Maryamiyya' enters the name

The order formally becomes the Tariqa Shadhiliyya-Darqawiyya-Alawiyya-Maryamiyya.

1969 ?
RCC

Arrival in Brazil (movement's account)

US Jesuit Fr. Haroldo Rahm begins 'Prayer Courses and Studies on the Holy Spirit' in Campinas in the second half of 1969 — RCC Brasil cites a 15–17 August 1969 experience with about 60 young people — and Brazil's first prayer group is born. The academic literature diverges: Prandi dates the movement's Brazilian take-off to the mid-1970s, while Souza (2008), citing Prandi (1997) and Carranza (2000), writes that the RCC 'arrived in Brazil between 1969 and 1970' with Rahm and Dougherty at Campinas; all datings are recorded.

1969
Gustavo Corção

A Tempo e Contratempo, Permanência's first house imprint; Fleichman's presidency begins

Permanência publishes Corção's A Tempo e Contratempo under its own imprint. Júlio Fleichman presides over Permanência and its editora from 1969 until 2003/04, per Permanência's own account.

1969
Gustavo Corção

The Colégio Sion campaign: 'Colégios católicos que se fecham'

Opens the campaign around the Colégio Sion with the crônica 'Colégios católicos que se fecham' — 'com conseqüências nefastas para a instituição', in Jalles de Paula's assessment.

1969
Saints

The Congregation of Rites is divided; the Congregation for the Causes of Saints is created

Paul VI's apostolic constitution Sacra Rituum Congregatio put two dicasteries in place of the Sacred Congregation of Rites, giving one the regulation of divine worship and the other the causes of saints. Earlier that year, on 19 March, his apostolic letter Sanctitas clarior had reduced the proof-gathering to a single cognitional process conducted by the bishop. Every act in this chronology before 1969 is an act of the Congregation of Rites; every act after it belongs to the new dicastery.

1969
Gustavo Corção

Campaign against the Dominicans; convergence with former adversaries of the TFP milieu

After the arrests of the Dominican friars, Corção campaigns against the order. Jalles de Paula: 'Corção não estava sozinho nessa batalha: a TFP e o grupo da revista Hora Presente comungavam as mesmas idéias. Unindo-se a antigos adversários, Corção rompia mais uma vez com o conservador que fora nos anos 1950.' That is a convergence with former 1950s adversaries, in Jalles's framing — not a rupture with the TFP, of which no document was found; see the disambiguation section for the three framings of the Corção–TFP relation.

1969
FSSPX

Paul VI presents the new Order of Mass

At his general audience days before the reformed Order of Mass takes effect, Paul VI tells the faithful that “nulla è mutato nella sostanza della nostra Messa tradizionale” (“nothing has been changed in the substance of our traditional Mass”), and that “il rito e la rubrica relativa non sono di per sé una definizione dogmatica” (“the rite and its rubric are not of themselves a dogmatic definition”). Both sentences are quoted on opposing sides of the later argument over the reform.

1970s

1970
TFP

Sigaud breaks with the TFP

Archbishop Sigaud breaks with Plinio and the movement, supporting the Médici government's agrarian program and diverging on liturgical-ecclesial questions.

1970
Gustavo Corção

Document by 200+ laymen to the CNBB assembly

A document signed by more than 200 laymen headed by Corção is sent to the CNBB assembly denouncing Marxist infiltration in the Church; it is not discussed, 'por falta de tempo hábil'.

1970
FSSPX

Founding of the SSPX

Bishop François Charrière erects the Society as a pia unio on an ad experimentum basis for six years; Marcel Lefebvre is founder and superior.

1971
FSSPX

Écône seminary opens

The International Seminary of Saint Pius X receives its first cohort of 24 candidates; 32 more enter in October 1972.

1971
Liberation Theology

Gutiérrez publishes 'Teología de la liberación'

The movement's foundational text; English translation 'A Theology of Liberation' follows in 1973 (Orbis).

1971
Liberation Theology

Casaldáliga becomes bishop

Paul VI named Pedro Casaldáliga prelate of São Félix and titular bishop of Altava on 27 August 1971; he received episcopal ordination on 23 October 1971 (principal consecrator Fernando Gomes dos Santos, Archbishop of Goiânia), beginning his emblematic 'church of the poor' prelature in the Araguaia (served 1971–2005). On the same occasion he issued his first pastoral letter, 'Uma Igreja da Amazônia em conflito com o latifúndio e a marginalização social', denouncing land conflict and the marginalization of Indigenous peoples and peasants — printed clandestinely under the dictatorship.

1972 ?
RCC

National diffusion begins

Rahm publishes 'Sereis Batizados no Espírito', presenting 'Catholic Pentecostalism'; Fr. Eduardo Dougherty SJ, ordained 1971, returns to Brazil and spreads retreats and prayer groups across the country (movement's account).

1972
Gustavo Corção

First public clash with Dom Eugênio Sales

Corção's 'Dialogando com o meu Pastor' (27 January 1972) opens the first public clash with the new archbishop of Rio. Roberto Marinho publicly intercedes for 'o respeito à hierarquia' (O Globo, 5 February 1972, via Jalles de Paula).

1972
Liberation Theology

Christians for Socialism encounter

First Latin American Encounter of Cristianos por el Socialismo (~400 delegates); the movement had begun in April 1971 when some 80 Chilean priests declared support for socialism.

1972
CELAM

The Sucre Assembly

The XIV Ordinary Assembly restructures the departments, self-defines CELAM as an 'organism of communion and service', and elects Alfonso López Trujillo secretary general (to 1979) — widely read by scholars as an institutional turning point; that reading is attributed.

1972
Liberation Theology

López Trujillo elected CELAM secretary-general

Alfonso López Trujillo was elected secretary-general of CELAM at Sucre, Bolivia, on 22 November 1972; from this post (and the CELAM presidency from 1979) he became, in the widely held reading, the leading intra-Latin-American institutional opponent of liberation theology.

1973 ?
TFP

The international network

Sister TFPs form in Argentina, Chile and Uruguay (1967), then Spain (1971), the United States (incorporated 18 May 1973 as the Foundation for a Christian Civilization — PT Wikipedia says 1974; the divergence is recorded), Canada (1975), France (1977), Germany (1982) and beyond.

1973
Liberation Theology

Assmann's 'Teología desde la praxis de la liberación'

Hugo Assmann published (Ediciones Sígueme, Salamanca) 'Teología desde la praxis de la liberación', a reworking of earlier texts that became one of the founding and most radical methodological statements of the current, proposing the liberating [[praxis]] of the poor as the starting point of theology.

1973 ?
RCC

First National Congress of the RCC

About 50 leaders meet, organized by Dougherty, Rahm and Sr. Juliette Schuckenbrock; a second congress follows in Belo Horizonte in 1974 (movement's account). The CNLB's affiliate profile likewise places the first congress in mid-1973 in Campinas and the second in January 1974.

1973
Gustavo Corção

O Século do Nada (Record)

Publishes O Século do Nada with Record — the book of his anti-Maritain turn — awarded the Instituto Nacional do Livro prize in 1974.

1973
Gustavo Corção

Second clash with the hierarchy; the Curia's note against him

Corção, 22 January 1973: 'são os Bispos… que estão comandando a destruição da autoridade… a comunização, a pulverização da Igreja'. The Rio Curia's note (O Globo, 2 February 1973) exhorts the faithful 'a não seguirem, em matéria religiosa, orientação de pessoas que… hoje se excedem'; a desagravo in Corção's support with more than 100 signatures follows (O Globo, 10 February 1973). All via Jalles de Paula's citations of the O Globo files.

1973
Liberation Theology

Chilean bishops ban Christians for Socialism

The Chilean episcopate's April 1973 plenary condemned clergy participation in Cristianos por el Socialismo; the resulting declaration 'Fe cristiana y actuación política', signed by Cardinal Raúl Silva Henríquez, was delayed by the 11 September coup and published on 16 October 1973, barring priests and religious from the movement. The substantive decision thus predates the coup, even though the formal text appeared after it.

1974
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Huston Smith joins

The scholar of world religions joins the order after meeting Schuon, attending his first [[dhikr]] in Tehran — with Nasr, one of the two most famous academic followers per Sedgwick.

1974
CELAM

ITEPAL opens

The four regional formation institutes — including IPLA (Quito), absorbed per the Sucre restructuring — are merged into ITEPAL, which moves to Bogotá in 1989.

1974
RCC

The first Malines Document

Cardinal Léon-Joseph Suenens convenes theologians (drafting by Kilian McDonnell) and publishes 'Malines Document 1' — the theological-pastoral orientations that gave the movement doctrinal respectability.

1974
Gustavo Corção

Rupture with the Brazilian Benedictine Congregation

A public exchange in the Jornal do Brasil of 1 July 1974 marks the official rupture between Corção — a Benedictine oblate since his conversion years — and the Congregação Beneditina Brasileira: 'romperiam oficialmente suas relações na década de 1970' (Jalles de Paula).

1974
FSSPX

Canonical visitation

Two Belgian priests inspect the seminary on behalf of a commission of cardinals.

1974
FSSPX

Lefebvre's Declaration

Lefebvre professes adherence to 'eternal Rome' while rejecting 'the Rome of neo-modernist tendencies' — the text that became the Society's ideological charter. The date is verified against the official text published by the SSPX General House, dated 21 November 1974.

1974 ?
Gustavo Corção

Progressive blindness; reported contacts with Archbishop Lefebvre and Dom Castro Mayer

The DHBB records his progressive loss of sight in the 1970s, narrowing his writing to Church subjects. The Centro Dom Bosco — a sympathetic traditionalist source, and the only one reached for these claims — reports a meeting with Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre in the early 1970s and collaboration with Dom Antônio de Castro Mayer, calling Corção 'decisivo na preservação da Missa de Sempre no Brasil'. Single-source, attributed, unverified.

1975 ?
Liberation Theology

Segundo publishes 'La liberación de la teología'

Uruguayan Jesuit Juan Luis Segundo published 'La liberación de la teología' (English: 'The Liberation of Theology', trans. John Drury, Orbis Books, 1976), setting out his methodological 'hermeneutic circle' — a repeated movement from lived reality to ideological suspicion of received theology and back to a renewed reading of Scripture.

1975
Perennialism

'Logic and Transcendence'

Schuon's fullest engagement with modern philosophy (French 'Logique et transcendance', Les Éditions Traditionnelles, 1970; English 1975). The 1970 French first edition is confirmed by the official Schuon bibliography.

1975 ?
Liberation Theology

First Interecclesial Meeting of the CEBs

The first national encounter of comunidades eclesiais de base gathered about 70 delegates from a dozen states, growing out of a January 1974 conversation involving Frei Betto, auxiliary bishop Luiz Gonzaga Fernandes and historian Eduardo Hoornaert. Betto became a leading organizer of these periodic meetings and a widely read popularizer of the CEBs. (Movement records; exact dates not archival.)

1975
FSSPX

Approval withdrawn

Bishop Pierre Mamie withdraws the Society's pia unio status with the approval of the Congregation for Religious and Paul VI; the SSPX disputes the legality of the procedure.

1975
RCC

Paul VI: 'a chance for the Church'

At the movement's third international congress in the Holy Year — with a Pentecost Mass in St Peter's on 18 May — Paul VI, addressing the participants in French on 19 May, asks: «Comment alors ce "renouveau spirituel" ne pourrait-il pas être une "chance" pour l'Église et pour le monde?» ('How then could this "spiritual renewal" not be a "chance" for the Church and for the world?'). The address was delivered in French; on vatican.va the Italian and English pages carry no body text, and the French page holds the authoritative original.

1975
Medjugorje

Romanis Pontificibus: the decree behind the 'Herzegovina affair'

Paul VI's decree Romanis Pontificibus orders the Herzegovinian Franciscans to cede most parishes of the Diocese of Mostar-Duvno to diocesan clergy; many friars refuse. The resulting standoff between the province and the bishops of Mostar — the 'Herzegovina affair' — is the institutional conflict already burning when the Medjugorje reports begin six years later, and the frame within which critics (and Bishop Žanić himself) read the Franciscan-run parish's embrace of the phenomenon. The decree text is cited from a document-archive blog, there being no primary hosting online.

1975
Liberation Theology

Comissão Pastoral da Terra (CPT) founded

The National Conference of Brazilian Bishops (CNBB) founded the Comissão Pastoral da Terra on 22 June 1975 at a pastoral meeting in Goiânia, to accompany squatters (posseiros), rural labourers and Indigenous peoples in land conflicts — an institutional expression of the land-and-poor pastoral line associated with figures such as Casaldáliga.

1976
FSSPX

The Palmar consecrations — an Écône personnel link

Fr. Maurice Revaz, canon of the Grand-Saint-Bernard and canon-law professor at Écône, drives Archbishop Ngô Đình Thục from Italy to El Palmar de Troya (arriving Christmas Eve 1975). On 11 January 1976 Thục consecrates five Palmarian bishops — declared excommunicated by the nuncio to Spain on 15 January — and on 28 January Revaz himself is consecrated a Palmarian bishop by Clemente Domínguez and others, placing him in the Palmarian, not the Thục, lineage; he leaves the movement about two years later (Lundberg). Lefebvre took no part; testimony reported by Lundberg has him declining an invitation to Palmar and pointing the group to Thục — attributed testimony, not established fact.

1976
FSSPX

The 'hot summer' ordinations

Lefebvre ordains priests despite an explicit prohibition, after being publicly rebuked by Paul VI in consistory on 24 May.

1976
FSSPX

Suspension a divinis

After a 1 July suspension from conferring orders and continued refusal to comply, Lefebvre is suspended from all sacramental ministry.

1976
FSSPX

Lefebvre's Lille sermon

Weeks after his suspension a divinis, Lefebvre preaches in a hangar at the Lille sports palace to a crowd La Porte Latine puts at about 7,000. Of the reformed rites he says: “Le rite de la nouvelle messe est un rite bâtard. Les sacrements sont des sacrements bâtards” — ‘the rite of the new Mass is a bastard rite; the sacraments are bastard sacraments’ — and “Les prêtres qui sortent des séminaires sont des prêtres bâtards.” The epithet falls on the rite, the sacraments and the priests; the sermon does not call the Mass itself ‘bastard’, a distinction later paraphrases have not always kept.

1976
FSSPX

Paul VI receives Lefebvre at Castel Gandolfo

Paul VI receives Lefebvre privately at the papal villa. According to the record kept by the assistant secretary of state who attended, published in 2018 by Mons. Leonardo Sapienza in ‘La Barca di Paolo’ and reported by Catholic News Service, the Pope told him: “Unfortunately, the position you have taken is that of an antipope.” The line is often relayed as having been sent in a letter; on this account it was spoken at this meeting.

1976
Guadalupe

The new basilica is opened and the image transferred

The new Basilica of Santa María de Guadalupe, built from 1974 to designs by Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, José Luis Benlliure, Alejandro Schoenhofer, Fray Gabriel Chávez de la Mora and Javier García Lascuráin, received the image in solemn procession on 12 October 1976; the eighteenth-century building it replaced had become structurally unsafe as the ground beneath it subsided. The circular plan seats about ten thousand.

1977
FSSPX

Guérard des Lauriers dismissed from Écône

Fr. Michel-Louis Guérard des Lauriers OP, professor of theology at the seminary from 1971, is dismissed in autumn 1977 after teaching the vacant-see thesis to seminarians in a September retreat despite Lefebvre's prohibition — the SSPX account (Si Si No No, 1998) and his sedeprivationist biographer (IMBC) agree on the dismissal and its ground. Consecrated by Thục in 1981, he became the source of the [[sedeprivationism|sedeprivationist]] ('Cassiciacum') lineages — see the episcopal genealogy.

1977
Olavo de Carvalho

First contributions to Folha de S.Paulo

Both Wikipedias date his first Folha de S.Paulo piece (in the Folhetim section, on The Magic Flute) to February 1977. Other sources push his journalism back earlier — Gazeta do Povo says he started at 17 at Folha da Manhã; Calil says he began writing for major newspapers in 1967. The sources do not reconcile these, and they may describe different roles. His own telling in the course: working from age 14 to ease his mother's burden, and entering journalism at 17 expressly because the legal five-hour day left time to study (lecture of 13 November 2010, among others), with the professional registry later obtained by time of service.

1977
Liberation Theology

Murder of Rutilio Grande

Jesuit priest Rutilio Grande, an advocate of peasant organizing and a friend of the newly appointed Archbishop Romero, was ambushed and killed with two companions. Many biographers (following Brockman) treat the killing as the catalyst of Romero's public turn; others (Morozzo della Rocca) argue his stance evolved from his pastoral formation. Both readings are attributed.

1977 ?
Our Lady of Tears

Death of Sister Amália de Jesus Flagelado

Sister Amália dies in Taubaté, São Paulo, aged 75; Santos (2019) writes that she died “com fama de santa”. Devotional sources and Tonon agree that she is buried in the tomb of the Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado in the Franciscan cemetery beside the Igreja de Santa Clara in Taubaté, not in the convent, and that the grave is marked and visited.

1977
Liberation Theology

Somoza's National Guard destroys the Solentiname community

After Solentiname members joined the FSLN assault on the San Carlos barracks (13 October 1977), Somoza's National Guard razed Cardenal's contemplative art-and-Gospel community in retaliation and he went into exile in Costa Rica. The community's collective Gospel readings had been published as 'El Evangelio en Solentiname', a landmark text of the current.

1978
RCC

Canção Nova founded

Fr. (later Msgr.) Jonas Abib founds the Canção Nova community — the future media powerhouse of the Brazilian Renewal.

1978
Aparecida

The image is seized and shattered

During evening devotions, Rogério Marcos de Oliveira, aged 19, seizes the image from its niche; in the struggle it falls and shatters into some 200 fragments, about 165 of them from the body. Characterizations of the perpetrator — 'Protestant iconoclast', accounts of his motives and later fate — vary across sources and are theirs, not this dataset's.

1978
Gustavo Corção

Death of Gustavo Corção

Dies on 6 July 1978. Jornal do Brasil and O Globo carry the death on 7 July; Alceu Amoroso Lima — his adversary of fifteen years — 'lhe dedicou um obituário belíssimo no Jornal do Brasil' (Jalles de Paula). The Portuguese Wikipedia adds death in Cosme Velho and a seventh-day mass on 14 July 1978 at São Judas Tadeu attended by Alceu, Sobral Pinto and military figures — plausible but resting on Wikipedia's citation of O Globo archives, so that detail stays flagged.

1978
Aparecida

The restoration at MASP is completed

Maria Helena Chartuni, head of the restoration department of the Museu de Arte de São Paulo, completes the reassembly and restoration of the shattered image — a jigsaw of some 200 fragments — begun on 28 June under director Pietro Maria Bardi. The image returns to Aparecida in an open fire-brigade car through crowds along the route.

1978
Cimbres

Fr. José Kehrle dies at Buíque

Kehrle — born at Rheinstetten, Germany, on 19 May 1891, in Brazil from 1909, ordained in 1914, made a monsignor in 1964 — had been moved to Buíque in 1947, where he served as priest and unpaid physician and built a chapel, a school and a maternity hospital, all under the title Nossa Senhora das Graças. He dies there on 6 August 1978, the anniversary of the first reported apparition, and is buried in the chapel he built. He never published the report he wrote in 1941; the coincidence of the date is a staple of the devotional accounts and is recorded here as a date, not as a sign.

1978 ?
FSSPX

Lefebvre meets John Paul II

Shortly after the papal election, Lefebvre seeks regularization; talks continue intermittently through the 1980s.

1979
Olavo de Carvalho

Co-founds the Escola Júpiter de Astrologia

Worked professionally as an astrologer c. 1979–1982, co-founding the school and its Revista de Astrologia Júpiter and collaborating on a university-extension astrology course at PUC-SP; Calil (2021) reports the school had about 140 students by 1980. He later abandoned and disavowed astrology. His own telling in the course: professionally an astrologer for about two years, with some 5,000 charts interpreted — psychiatrists sent him patients' horoscopes — and the school closing around 1980; he quit after judging the techniques unserious.

1979
Liberation Theology

CELAM III (Puebla)

John Paul II's opening address (28 January) warns against 're-readings' of Christ as political revolutionary while affirming the defense of human dignity; the conference document consolidates the 'preferential option for the poor'.

1979
CELAM

III General Conference (Puebla)

Opened by John Paul II on 28 January; concluding document on evangelization in Latin America's present and future.

1980s

1980
FSSPX

Latin American seminary founded

The Seminario Nuestra Señora Corredentora is founded in the Buenos Aires priory and moves to La Reja in 1981 — the formation house for Latin America, including Brazil.

1980

Schuon moves to Bloomington

Schuon and his wife emigrate from Lausanne to Bloomington, where a large community of disciples already lived; the publishing house World Wisdom is founded there the same year.

1980
Perennialism

'Temenos' launches

Kathleen Raine, Keith Critchlow, Brian Keeble and Philip Sherrard found the review devoted to 'the arts of the imagination' — seed of the Temenos Academy (1990), of which the Prince of Wales (now King Charles III) has been patron since its founding.

1980
RCC

Associação do Senhor Jesus

Fr. Eduardo Dougherty founds the Associação do Senhor Jesus: publications, music and the TV program 'Anunciamos o Senhor'. The same year Raniero Cantalamessa OFMCap, a preacher close to the Renewal, is appointed Preacher of the Papal Household by John Paul II — 1980 is confirmed by the Holy See Press Office biography ('nominato da Giovanni Paolo II Predicatore della Casa Pontificia nel 1980'), an independent corroboration of what had been a CHARIS-only dating.

1980 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

First book: A Imagem do Homem na Astrologia

The 1980 date belongs to a 38-page booklet (Ed. Júpiter), grown out of the 1979 PUC-SP astrology course. Olavo's own CV opens its 'Livros publicados' list with three 1983 titles (Speculum) — which explains Agência Brasil's 'first book, 1983': the two dates describe different things (a course booklet vs. his first books proper). The 1980 edition is attested only by book-trade listings, so the flag stays.

1980 ?
Gustavo Corção

Posthumous memoirs: Conversa em Sol Menor

His memoirs Conversa em Sol Menor appear posthumously. The 1980 year rests on a single sympathetic source (an admirer's chronology), so the flag stays.

1980
Liberation Theology

Assassination of Óscar Romero

A day after a homily ordering soldiers, 'in the name of God', to cease the repression, Romero was shot dead while celebrating Mass. The 1993 UN Truth Commission for El Salvador found that former Major Roberto D'Aubuisson had given the order to kill the archbishop; the finding is attributed to that report.

1980
Aparecida

Law 6.802/1980 makes 12 October a national holiday in honour of the Patroness

Brazil's Congress enacts Law 6.802 of 30 June 1980, declaring 12 October a national holiday in honour of Nossa Senhora Aparecida, Patroness of Brazil. The holiday is the civil act that fixed 12 October in the national calendar — a date no colonial source attests for the finding itself.

1980
Aparecida

John Paul II consecrates the new basilica

On the first papal visit to the shrine, John Paul II consecrates the new basilica — 'I come, therefore, to consecrate this Basilica', his homily says — and the building receives the title of minor basilica. The homily retells the finding of 1717 and recalls the 1978 breakage and restoration. A consecration dedicates a building for worship; the pope's retelling of the tradition is a devotional act, not a historical finding.

1981 ?
Liberation Theology

Boff publishes 'Igreja: Carisma e Poder'

Franciscan theologian Leonardo Boff published 'Igreja: Carisma e Poder — Ensaios de eclesiologia militante' (Editora Vozes), whose critique of the Church's institutional power structure prompted the CDF investigation that led to the 1984 colloquy and the 1985 Notification; it appeared in English as 'Church: Charism and Power' (Crossroad, 1985).

1981
Perennialism

Nasr's Gifford Lectures

Seyyed Hossein Nasr delivers the Gifford Lectures, published as 'Knowledge and the Sacred' — per the Gifford records, the first Muslim and first non-Westerner to do so: the school's academic high-water mark.

1981
Guadalupe

Claims about the tilma and the image are published by named researchers

The biophysicist Philip Serna Callahan published The Tilma under Infra-Red Radiation as monograph no. 3 of the CARA Studies on Popular Devotion (Georgetown University, 1981), reporting infrared photography of the cloth and arguing that parts of the image show no underdrawing or sizing. Separately, the engineer José Aste Tönsmann (1931–2024) worked on digitised enlargements of the image's eyes from 1979 and asserted that thirteen human figures are reflected in them. Both are CLAIMS by named individuals, dated and attributed here — neither was published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal, both are rejected by critics as over-reading, and this dataset takes no position on either. No Church act recorded in this chronology rules on the origin or composition of the image.

RCC

John Paul II receives the leaders' conference

The pope addresses the fourth international leaders' conference of the Renewal — the first of his repeated receptions.

1981
Medjugorje

First reported sighting

On the feast of St. John the Baptist, two — then four — young people report seeing a luminous female figure on the hill Podbrdo (on the slope called Crnica) and identify her as the Virgin Mary. This is the first reported sighting; the shrine's official anniversary is the following day, when the group of six first reported seeing her together. Both dates circulate as 'the' date in different sources, and this chronology carries both events explicitly.

1981
Medjugorje

The six report their first apparition together — the anniversary date

Vicka Ivanković, Mirjana Dragićević, Ivan Dragičević, Marija Pavlović, Ivanka Ivanković and Jakov Čolo report seeing the figure together for the first time. 25 June is the date the shrine keeps as the anniversary of the apparitions, and the date of Ivanka's reported annual apparition since 1985.

1981
Medjugorje

Crowds gather; the reported message of 'peace'; police and doctors respond

Some 2,000 people gather on the hill. The seers report the figure's words, summarized in the tradition as 'peace, peace, and only peace', and the title 'Queen of Peace' (Kraljica Mira) enters the reports — reported, never Church-authenticated as to origin. In the following days (27–29 June, per the accounts gathered by sympathetic chroniclers) the communist authorities interview the children and have them medically and psychiatrically examined at Mostar; the examinations are reported to have found them sound. The examination reports themselves are known through the devotional literature, not through located records.

1981 ?
Medjugorje

Bishop Žanić's first public words: 'the children are not lying' — spoken statements, not an act

Preaching at a confirmation in Medjugorje, Bishop Pavao Žanić of Mostar-Duvno says: 'I am convinced: the children are not lying!' In a Glas Koncila interview published 16 August 1981 he adds: 'It is definite that the children were not incited by anyone, and especially not by the church, to lie.' These are informal statements of the ordinary about the seers' sincerity, not acts of judgment on the apparitions — and within three years the same bishop will become the phenomenon's most vocal episcopal critic. The reversal has two competing explanations in the literature (police pressure per sympathetic sources; the Ivica Vego affair and the taped early interviews per critical ones), recorded here as perspectives.

1981
Medjugorje

Pastor Jozo Zovko arrested; the state moves against the gatherings

Fr. Jozo Zovko OFM, pastor of St. James — initially skeptical of the children, by now their defender — is arrested by the Yugoslav authorities; the friary is searched, and the state moves to suppress the gatherings on Podbrdo. The apparition reports continue in the church and elsewhere.

1981 ?
Medjugorje

Zovko tried and sentenced

In a one-day trial at Mostar, Fr. Jozo Zovko is convicted over a sermon read as a 'nationalist plot' against the socialist state and sentenced to imprisonment — reported as 3 or 3.5 years depending on the source — and released early, reportedly in February 1983. The prosecution of the pastor, weeks into the reports, fixed the phenomenon's early framing as a confrontation between the parish and the communist state.

1982
FSSPX

Schmidberger succeeds Lefebvre

Fr. Franz Schmidberger becomes Superior General (1982–1994); Lefebvre remains the Society's episcopal head.

1982 ?
Knights of Columbus

The centennial convention

President Ronald Reagan addresses the Order's 100th Supreme Convention in its centennial year, and John Paul II sends Cardinal Agostino Casaroli as his personal envoy — per the Order's timeline, which places around the same era its Vatican partnership projects, from satellite uplinks (1975) to the restoration of St. Peter's facade (1985).

1982 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

Enters the Traditionalist / Perennialist milieu

Per Calil (2021), around 1982 he joined an esoteric 'Tradition' group. Sedgwick's peer-reviewed history of Brazilian Traditionalism (2021) documents the arc: an interest in Traditionalism from 1977, after he was given Jacob Needleman's anthology The Sword of Gnosis; Michel Veber's 1980 lecture on Guénon at the Escola Júpiter and a further series in 1981; his own translation of Guénon's La Métaphysique orientale (A Metafísica Oriental, Escola Júpiter, 1981); an article in the New Age magazine Planeta (no. 107, August 1981) calling Veber 'my professor' — in which he also wrote that Veber had studied under Guénon's personal guidance, which Sedgwick notes was impossible, Veber having been four years old when Guénon left France in 1930; the founding of an Instituto de Estudos Tradicionais; and the first Traditionalist article to appear in a major Brazilian newspaper, 'Moralidade sem Deus?' (Jornal da Tarde, 27 February 1982). He also led followers into A Tradição, the organization of Omar Ali Shah, and broke with it after concluding its leader was a 'con-man' (Sedgwick, quoting his term) — a public dispute that reached the police. Approximate dating for the group entry itself; all characterizations are these scholars'.

RCC

Comunidade Católica Shalom founded

Moysés Louro de Azevedo Filho opens the 'evangelizing snack bar' in Fortaleza, with Cardinal Lorscheider present — birth of the Shalom community.

1982 ?
Medjugorje

Mirjana reports her tenth secret; her daily apparitions end

Mirjana Dragićević reports receiving the tenth of the ten secrets and, per the reported structure, her daily apparitions cease — the first of the six for whom this happens. She reports thereafter an annual apparition on 18 March, and, from 1987, apparitions on the 2nd of each month 'for unbelievers'.

1982 ?
Medjugorje

Fr. Tomislav Vlašić at the parish — the contested 'spiritual director'

Fr. Tomislav Vlašić OFM serves at the Medjugorje parish and becomes widely described as the seers' first 'spiritual director' — the label used in CDF-related reporting decades later, and disputed by the Mostar chancery and by critics as to how formal the role ever was. He leaves for Italy in the 1980s and founds a mixed community there; the Church acts about him (2008, 2009, 2020) are acts about the man and are recorded separately in this chronology.

1982 ?
Medjugorje

The diocesan commissions investigate — and the 1986 vote is negative, per the bishop's own account

Bishop Žanić establishes a four-member diocesan commission of inquiry in 1982 and enlarges it to fifteen members in 1984. Its final vote, per Žanić's 1990 statement: 11 members found nothing supernatural, 2 (Franciscans) held the apparitions authentic, 1 found something 'in nucleo', 1 abstained. The commission's report was never published — the vote is known only through the bishop's published account, and this dataset cites it as such.

1982
RCC

Sword of the Spirit federation

A federation of covenant communities grows out of the Word of God (unified association 1982, international entity 1983; 82 communities, 45 Catholic, by 2017). In 1990 the Word of God disaffiliated from the Sword of the Spirit — per Wikipedia and contemporary press, following an order by Bishop Albert Ottenweller after a review of the community's governance. Contemporary press (Christianity Today, 16 Sept 1991; National Catholic Reporter, 18 April 1997) described 'heavy shepherding'/'discipleship' practices; in 1991 co-founder Ralph Martin acknowledged the Sword of the Spirit disciplines had been 'an ill-advised venture' that was 'especially harmful to women' and 'fostered elitism'. A faction led by Stephen Clark then formed a separate community keeping Sword of the Spirit affiliation. These are documented criticisms and a founder's own retrospective, labeled as such — not the site's characterization.

1983
FSSPX

The 'Nine' leave — SSPV founded

Fr. Clarence Kelly and eight US priests are expelled from or leave the SSPX in a dispute over the 1962 Missal and openness to sedevacantism, founding the Society of Saint Pius V — a distinct, sedevacantist-leaning body. Fr. Anthony Cekada, one of the nine, dates the break to their letter of 25 March 1983 and the Oyster Bay meeting of 27 April 1983, by which time Lefebvre had removed Fr. Donald Sanborn as rector of the Ridgefield seminary and installed Fr. Richard Williamson. The Society's own June 1983 Angelus editorial, defending the Archbishop, also attacked the Ridgefield faculty — singling out the presence of the perennialist writer Dr. Rama Coomaraswamy and 'the endorsement given to his dangerous book in the Seminary journal' as 'sufficient to remove any vestige of intellectual credibility from Ridgefield'; Cekada's own account of the dispute does not mention him.

1983
Perennialism

Lings's 'Muhammad'

Martin Lings's prize-winning biography becomes the school's biggest crossover into mainstream Islamic readership.

1983
Saints

Divinus perfectionis Magister recasts the law of causes

John Paul II's apostolic constitution rebuilt the procedure: the inquiry is conducted by the diocesan bishop, its material is worked up into a Positio under a relator using critical hagiographical method, and medical experts examine an alleged cure separately from the examination of virtues. The Code of Canon Law was promulgated the same day. This is the main reason the older and newer paths in this chronology do not look alike — Labouré's and Soubirous' causes ran under the Congregation of Rites, the Martos' and Lúcia's under the reformed procedure.

1983
Liberation Theology

John Paul II reprimands Ernesto Cardenal

As John Paul II arrived in Nicaragua on 4 March 1983, Culture Minister Cardenal knelt to greet him; the pope wagged his finger and admonished him to 'regularize' his situation with the Church — an image emblematic of the Vatican's clash with priests in the Sandinista government.

Aparecida

The National Conference of Bishops of Brazil declares the new basilica the National Shrine of Nossa Senhora Aparecida. The declaration is well-attested in secondary accounts but the primary act has not been located, and the year itself is disputed between the shrine's own page (1983) and most other accounts (1984).

Liberation Theology

Cardenal suspended a divinis

Ernesto Cardenal is suspended from priestly ministry under John Paul II for holding government office.

1984
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Nasr's Washington platform

Seyyed Hossein Nasr becomes University Professor of Islamic Studies at George Washington University and founds the Foundation for Traditional Studies, publisher of the journal Sophia.

1984
TFP

The Venezuelan crisis

A press campaign and parliamentary inquiry target the 'Resistencia-TFP', with a member arrested on suspicion of a plot against John Paul II and accusations of being a 'sect'; the accusations never produced a conviction in the sources consulted, the Venezuelan TFP was effectively extinguished by around 1986, and every characterization here is attributed.

1984 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

Involvement with Sufi Islam (the Maryamiyya of Frithjof Schuon)

Calil (2021) states he converted to Islam in 1984, took the name Sidi Mohammad Ibrahim, and set up a tariqa of his own that dissolved within months. Sedgwick (2021) documents the sequence from the Lings correspondence: after contacting Marco Pallis, Carvalho and some of his followers corresponded with Martin Lings, who explained that 'entering Islam' was the appropriate preparation for the Maryami tariqa — which they did. Lings's letters address him as 'Sidi Muhammad' from June 1985; they were deposited at the São Paulo state court in 1990 as evidence in a case brought by a former follower, which Carvalho won. Sedgwick places him at the 1985 Traditionalist conference in Lima where Lings was present, but marks that presence as deduced rather than documented; he records that in 1986 Carvalho led a group of Brazilians to Bloomington, Indiana, where Schuon appointed him muqaddam (Sufi representative) for Brazil. Carvalho himself later said he had 'entered' Islam rather than converted, in Guénon's sense. On the exit Sedgwick is only relative — 'within a few years' he became disenchanted, telling an interviewer he had discovered that Schuon backed a rival for the leadership of the Brazilian Maryamiyya and had endorsed rumours against him — which converges with his own telling in the course: met Schuon in 1986, made muqaddam on arrival, stayed about a year and a half, provoked his own expulsion, placing the exit around 1987–88 (per the community transcriptions). He later disavowed the period's books as reflecting 'more a duty of obedience' to Schuon than his own thought.

1984 ?
Our Lady of Tears

Death of Madre Maria Villac

Maria Villac, in religion Madre Maria do Calvário, foundress and lifelong superior general of the Instituto das Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado, dies on 1 March 1984, aged 90. She outlived Sister Amália by nearly seven years.

1984 ?
Medjugorje

The reported message series begin: weekly to the parish (1984), monthly 'to the world' (1987)

From 1 March 1984, per the devotional record, the seers report weekly Thursday messages addressed to the parish; from 25 January 1987 the series becomes the monthly 'message to the world' reported through Marija Pavlović on the 25th of each month — the series the shrine still publishes. The 2024 DDF Note will later require that all such texts be presented as 'alleged messages' and subject future publication to the Apostolic Visitor's approval.

1984
Liberation Theology

CDF Instruction 'Libertatis Nuntius'

The Instruction on Certain Aspects of the 'Theology of Liberation', signed by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, warns against the uncritical borrowing of Marxist analysis while not condemning the theme of liberation itself.

1984
Liberation Theology

Boff's colloquy with the CDF over 'Church: Charism and Power'

Leonardo Boff was summoned to a colloquy at the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, meeting for about two hours with its prefect, Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, over his 1981 book 'Church: Charism and Power'. Brazilian cardinals Aloísio Lorscheider and Paulo Evaristo Arns attended in support; a Notification and a period of imposed silence followed in 1985.

1985 ?
Liberation Theology

Segundo's 'Theology and the Church' answers Cardinal Ratzinger

Juan Luis Segundo published 'Theology and the Church: A Response to Cardinal Ratzinger and a Warning to the Whole Church', a book-length rebuttal to the CDF's 1984 Instruction 'Libertatis Nuntius', arguing the critique rested on a dualistic theology at odds with Vatican II and missed what liberation theologians actually taught. It became a key primary document of the reception debate (reviewed by Avery Dulles, 1986).

1985
Cimbres

Irmã Adélia breaks a forty-nine-year silence

Diagnosed with breast cancer in 1984 and, on the accounts of those around her, given a short prognosis after metastasis, Irmã Adélia asks her superior's leave to tell her community about 1936 and then joins a congregational pilgrimage to Sítio Guarda — her first return. She abandons treatment afterwards and lives a further twenty-eight years. That she was cured is what the congregation, her companion Irmã Viviane and her secretary Ione Paiva report, and what the Catholic press repeats; it is recorded here as a report, with its reporters, and no Church act has declared it. From this point organised pilgrimages of hundreds begin arriving, the first from Fortaleza brought by Frei Marcos Queiroz Câmara, and the case enters public circulation for the first time since 1937.

Liberation Theology

Notification on Leonardo Boff

The CDF issues a Notification on Boff's 'Church: Charism and Power'; Boff is subsequently subjected to a period of imposed silence, lifted in 1986.

1985
TFP

The CNBB note

A CNBB commission (Bishops Gilberto Pereira Lopes, Antonio Misiara, Carlos Alberto Navarro) declares 'notorious the lack of communion of the TFP with the Church in Brazil', citing 'esoteric character, religious fanaticism, cult of the leader's personality and abusive use of the name of Mary', and exhorts Catholics not to join. The full text circulates mainly via the TFP's own replies — the CNBB original is a documented gap.

1985 ?
Liberation Theology

'Fidel e a Religião' (Fidel and Religion)

Frei Betto's long interviews with Fidel Castro, conducted in May 1985, were published as 'Fidel y la religión' (Havana, 1985; English 'Fidel and Religion', 1987). Some observers credit the book with helping open a dialogue between the Cuban revolution and religious believers; that assessment is contested and attributed, not asserted.

1985 ?
Cimbres

The earliest dated report of a cure attributed to the site

The earliest cure account the 2016 UNICAP study can date is Maria Eunice do Rego Barros's, from the pilgrimage of 31 August 1985; the study states in terms that it has no testimonies from the period of the apparitions themselves and that the reports begin here. Further accounts it collects are dated 1986 to 1989 and are attributed to named individuals. Against that stands Kehrle's own diary, which claims that a register kept at the Guarda in 1936 already held some six hundred testimonies — a register nobody in the reachable literature has produced. What is recorded here is that cures were reported, by whom and when: this dataset makes no finding that any cure occurred, and no bishop or dicastery has recognised one at Cimbres.

1986 ?
Liberation Theology

Boff brothers publish the liberation-theology primer

Clodovis and Leonardo Boff co-published 'Como fazer teologia da libertação' (Vozes), a widely translated methodological primer built on the see–judge–act method; it appeared in English as 'Introducing Liberation Theology' (Orbis, 1987). Clodovis would later, in 2007, publish a sharp epistemological self-critique of the movement.

1986 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Brazilian branch founded

Olavo de Carvalho leads a group of Brazilians to Bloomington, is initiated (taking the name Sidi Muhammad Ibrahim, per the critical documentary blog, corroborated in outline by Sedgwick), and is appointed [[muqaddam]] in Brazil under Martin Lings's guidance — described as the first tariqa brought to Brazil by Brazilians. Sources split between 1986 and 1987 for the founding: 1986 is the year the hostile Jardim series gives — and Carvalho's own first-person placing of himself in the United States in '86, below, is consistent with it — while Brazilian tertiary sources give 1987. Sedgwick's published abstract places the Brazilian episode only in the 1980s and 1990s without a year, and the full Aries 21:2 article remains the document expected to settle it. Sedgwick's later obituary notice (28 Jan 2022) calls him "briefly a Maryami and the [[khalifa]] of Frithjof Schuon in Brazil", where this dataset and the Brazilian sources say [[muqaddam]]; the two titles are distinct and the divergence is recorded, not resolved. Carvalho's own dated account, in the Curso Online de Filosofia, adds a fourth voice to this record without settling it. In aula 094 (12 February 2011) he said that as soon as he entered Schuon's tariqa he was immediately appointed its [[muqaddam]] — which he glossed as the organization's representative for Brazil — that he had not asked for the post and found the speed of the appointment odd, and that the order then passed its instructions to a different man, creating a conflict of authority he described as dishonest. In aula 033 (21 November 2009) he said that at the time of the essays collected in Universalidade e Abstração (1983) he was an aspirant who later became a member, that he wrote in the manner of the Études Traditionnelles circle in order to please the shaykh, and that he was accepted at the first attempt while others had been applying yearly for twelve years. His self-description as [[muqaddam]] agrees with this dataset and the Brazilian sources and not with Sedgwick's obituary notice, which says [[khalifa]]; a participant's self-disclosure does not adjudicate a scholarly source, and the divergence stays recorded rather than resolved. Two further dated lectures bear on how he came into the order. In aula 206 (1 June 2013) he said that in '86 he was in the United States for the first time and that he met Whitall Perry there — a first-person placing of himself in the country in the year the Jardim series gives, although in that passage he named neither Bloomington nor the initiation. In aula 279 (17 January 2015) he described the route in: he reached Schuon's tariqa first through the Buddhist author Marco Pallis, and then Rama P. Coomaraswamy — whom he called a traditionalist Catholic theologian and Schuon's second-closest collaborator — put him in contact with it; his stated reason for joining was that the order was multi-confessional, so that belonging to it would not commit him to another religion. His own entry is self-disclosure and is accepted as such; what he says there about Pallis and about Coomaraswamy is a claim about third parties, carried attributed and, in Coomaraswamy's case, against Sedgwick rather than with him. None of this is quoted: the corpus text is a community transcription unchecked against the audio.

1986
Olavo de Carvalho

Publishes Fronteiras da Tradição, his first book beyond astrology

Lectures from his Traditionalist teaching period — several first given at the Editora Astrociência in Rio de Janeiro — are published as Fronteiras da Tradição (São Paulo: Nova Stella, 1986), per Sedgwick (2021) his first book on a topic other than astrology. Its references are 'almost without exception' the major Traditionalist authors; it urges an orthodox religion as the exoteric frame for esoteric practice and warns against pseudo-initiatic organizations, citing A Tradição as an example. The volume also reprints the 1982 Jornal da Tarde article.

Cimbres

Reporting on Pesqueira politics in 2020, Brasil de Fato states that at the 1986 jubilee of the reported vision Bishop Manuel Palmeira da Rocha 'admitiu o testemunho das duas crianças como verdadeiro'. The Instruction on Christian Freedom and Liberation gives a more positive treatment of liberation themes.

In a letter dated 9 April 1986 to the Brazilian Episcopal Conference (CNBB), John Paul II writes that — developed in fidelity to Church doctrine and the two recent CDF Instructions — 'the theology of liberation is not only opportune, but useful and necessary' ('la teologia della liberazione è non solo opportuna, ma utile e necessaria'), a passage cited by the movement's defenders.

Cardinal Franjo Kuharić and Bishop Žanić jointly announce — in a communiqué published in Glas Koncila on 18 January — that at the CDF's suggestion the judgment of the Medjugorje events passes from the diocesan commission to a commission of the Yugoslav Bishops' Conference. 1987 ?
Olavo de Carvalho

The course his CV calls the seminar's beginning

His curriculum vitae lists a course called “Introdução à Vida Intelectual”, taught from March 1987 to March 1989 at the Instituto Sto. André in Rio de Janeiro, and adds that under the name Seminário Permanente de Filosofia e Humanidades it “continues to this day” in São Paulo. That is his own claim of an unbroken teaching line, and it is the only dated origin the seminar has: no independent record of a founding has been found, and the organization entry keeps the date unverified. It sits beside rather than against the “teaching since 1989” he gives in the online course — the Rio course ends in March 1989 and the renamed seminar takes over from there, so the two self-datings mark different starting points. The sentence carrying the claim is undated, like the rest of the document: the CV's sections were revised at different times, so “to this day” dates from whenever that paragraph was last touched. Its list of internal course handouts — forty-two fascicles under the Seminário Permanente name — is the only other trace of the course in it.

Mirjana (by then Mirjana Soldo) reports the start of apparitions on the 2nd of each month, described in the devotion as prayer 'for unbelievers'. The series will run — with large public gatherings at the Blue Cross in later years — until her announcement of its end on 18 March 2020.

1988 ?
Cimbres

The site is decreed a sanctuary — under a bishop whose dates do not fit the reported year

The shrine's own history states that Bishop Manoel Palmeira da Rocha of Pesqueira decreed the site the Santuário de Nossa Senhora das Graças on 20 October 1998, and the regional press repeats it. He cannot have: Palmeira da Rocha governed Pesqueira from 14 March 1980 until 26 May 1993, and the bishop in 1998 was Bernardino Marchiò. Ione Paiva, who was present, says instead that she and Irmã Adélia received the sanctuary licence from Palmeira da Rocha in 1988 and that his signed decree is reproduced in her book. Either the year or the bishop in the received account is wrong. This dataset records the act as falling in Palmeira da Rocha's episcopate, leaves the date unresolved, and notes that the decree text has not been reached — a copy is said to be printed in a book that is not sold.

Medjugorje

Marija in Alabama: the origin of the contested 'Caritas of Birmingham' connection

Marija Pavlović travels to the Birmingham area to donate a kidney to her brother, hosted by layman Terry Colafrancesco — the founder of 'Caritas of Birmingham' (Sterrett, Alabama), who styles himself 'A Friend of Medjugorje'. 1989 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Olavo breaks with Schuon

Olavo de Carvalho leaves the order after a rupture with Schuon; the sources disagree on the date — 1987 (Bunker da Cultura), 'the late 1980s' (Sedgwick), or an expelled-1987–91 window (the Jardim series) — and on the reasons, with his own published framing (the multiconfessional rule, in 'Influências discretas') differing from the hostile accounts; every version is attributed. He reinvents himself, in Sedgwick's phrase, as a Catholic-identifying 'post-Traditionalist'. Carvalho's own tellings in the Curso Online de Filosofia are a fourth attributed account, and they diverge from each other, which is itself the finding. In aula 094 (12 February 2011) he framed the ending as his own act — when he broke with these people, he said, they activated the order's customary judicial harassment, inventing a case accusing him of a crime he had not committed which cost him six years and nearly ruined him before he won it; he added that the accusation he suffered came at forty (he was born in 1947), and that all of it was more than twenty years earlier. In aula 218 (31 August 2013), reading out a critic's summary that had him expelled from the Maryamiyya, he said that this checked out perfectly, that he had already explained the circumstances, and that he holds the whole correspondence; in the same lecture he said that one cannot leave a tariqa of one's own accord — either the man puts you out or you never leave. In aula 241 (1 March 2014) he repeated the six-year lawsuit and said the order's usual practice was judicial harassment, which it could not press further in Brazil because it had only eight disciples there. A third dated telling, in aula 242 (15 March 2014), supplies a cause the 2013 one did not: he said he had left expelled from the [[tariqa]] for quarrelling with the shaykh, and in the same passage that the order he had belonged to was Schuon's, which hated Freemasonry and put out anyone who went near it. That passage also appears to give the length of his membership as a year and a half — a figure that does not sit easily with an entry in 1986 and a break at the end of the 1980s — but the sentence is elliptical in a transcription never checked against the audio, and it is recorded here as an open question rather than as a duration. In aula 279 (17 January 2015) he said that Schuon, late in life and under pressure from other tariqas, decreed that only Muslims would be accepted: a dated restatement of the multiconfessional rule that his own published account gives as his reason for leaving, although in the lecture he neither dates the decree nor ties it to his own departure. In letters of 28 and 30 January 2001, published by the traditionalist-Catholic Associação Cultural Montfort, Olavo de Carvalho defends René Guénon against Orlando Fedeli's charge of gnosticism: the spiritual elevation Guénon shows 'in numerous passages of his work', he writes, is enough to inhibit any disrespectful contestation, and Guénon's relation to Christianity cannot be reduced to that of an infiltrator in Catholic circles. Fedeli's reply of 3 February 2001 presses him on whether he is himself Catholic. Both positions are the correspondents' own, not this site's. The exchange dates the Brazilian reception: Carvalho was still publicly defending Guénon in 2001, fifteen years before the 2016 'As garras da Esfinge' reversed that position — when he left Schuon's order is an order-side question and belongs to the sibling cronologia/tariqa project.

Perennialism

The Prince of Wales addresses the Sacred Web conference

The Sacred Web conference on 'Tradition in the Modern World' meets at the Myer Horowitz Theatre, University of Alberta, Edmonton, on 23–24 September 2006, and opens with an introduction from the Prince of Wales — the future King Charles III — who says in it that he cannot be present, so the address is given in absentia and also exists as a video. It is the fullest public statement of his reading in this field, and unusually for this subject it is first-person rather than commentary. In it he speaks of the need for those who can speak of the eternal wisdom that is called the perennial philosophy; says he is always delighted to receive the latest issue of Sacred Web; states that through the work of the Temenos Academy, of which he is Patron, he has been fortunate to enjoy the writings of Seyyed Hossein Nasr and the late Martin Lings; notes that Temenos remains closely associated with many who write for Sacred Web, for Sophia and for related journals; and refers to the critique of the false premises of modernity set out in 'The Reign of Quantity'. Mark Sedgwick, writing on 1 October 2006, took the speech to show the Prince better read in Traditionalism than he had thought, and recorded that it nonetheless did not otherwise clarify his own personal position — which is the standard this chronology keeps. What the address establishes is reading, patronage and public praise, all self-disclosed; it establishes no membership, initiation or affiliation, and none is claimed here. The later commentary reads it the same way: Rod Dreher in The American Conservative (12 March 2012) calls him a devotee of the perennial philosophy on the strength of his public statements, and Theo Hobson in The Spectator (17 September 2022) places his outlook close to the perennial philosophy partly inspired by René Guénon — and neither asserts that he belongs to anything.

Read as concealment: the Traditionalist School in the Curso Online de Filosofia

Through the first three years of Olavo de Carvalho's Curso Online de Filosofia the school is treated as a body of thought whose defect, on his account, is not error but concealment — and the position is already fully formed in aula 005 (25 April 2009), three months before aula 015. Asked by a student whether Coomaraswamy, Guénon, Schuon and Evola fell into the same cognitive trap as Descartes, he answers that the trouble with the Traditionalist school is not cognitive but a matter of untruth: they are, he says, profound thinkers who speak on behalf of secret societies and so cannot tell the whole story, which is why a reader needs historical as well as imaginative equipment to reconstruct what is withheld. In the same lecture he separates the metaphysical Orient Guénon invokes from the historical one; notes that Guénon denounces the historical method while using it throughout Le Théosophisme; objects that the initiation Guénon's remedy relies on is an idealized Masonry rather than the historical one; reads the Sphinx pen-name as a demand to be deciphered rather than followed; argues that Guénon writes Eastern doctrine in the most characteristically French academic manner, so that reading Descartes is a prerequisite for reading him; and sets one prediction he counts right, the disappearance of cash, beside one he counts wrong, that China would never turn Bolshevik — a pairing he makes again in aula 146 (17 March 2012). By aula 131 (19 November 2011) the same charge is pressed with the transmission named. He rejects Guénon's account of symbolism as a primitive science preserved and handed on by esoteric organizations, on the ground that a natural symbolism could not be anyone's property; repeats Henri Massis's jibe that Guénon is wrapped up in his manvantaras; says he still cannot draw the line between where Guénon fails to understand and where he understands and conceals; and asks whether the shaykh he names as Guénon's initiator, Elish El-Kebir, did not have his own project of islamizing the West. Both lectures are Carvalho's characterization of the school, dated and attributed, and are recorded here as reception, never in this site's voice. Perennialism

The transcendent unity of religions granted, and bounded

The same course that carries Carvalho's 'islamization' reading also carries his assent to the doctrinal claim at the centre of the school, and the two run side by side without either displacing the other. In aula 028 (17 October 2009), answering a student on gnosis in Hans Jonas and Voegelin against gnosis in Schuon, he says that much of what Guénon and Schuon teach is compatible with Christianity and with the other religions insofar as they keep to a core of metaphysical knowledge genuinely common to them, and that the two said and proved that such a common set of metaphysical principles exists; his own objection, he insists, is not Orlando Fedeli's charge of gnosticism, which he says does not impress him. In aula 038 (26 December 2009) he states the limit: a common metaphysics does exist across the traditions and Guénon and Schuon proved it, but metaphysics is a description of the structure of reality and is therefore not religion; the religions promise something beyond that description and diverge precisely there; no standpoint stands above the set of them; and the same common metaphysics is also found outside religion altogether, in Aristotle, Plato and al-Fārābī. In aula 101 (9 April 2011) he names Schuon's transcendent unity of religions and calls the theory correct while reading it narrowly, as asserting only that the metaphysical doctrine underlying the religions is the same, and points to Whitall Perry's compilation for the documentation. In aula 151 (21 April 2012) the position has its settled form: all the religions have the same metaphysics, but metaphysics is a schema of reality and says nothing about God's action in time, which is where he locates Christianity's difference. Across those three years the assent does not move while the polemic sharpens — which is what a five-year course can show and a single clip cannot. The Carvalho–Dugin debate as a contest over the Traditionalist inheritance

The 2011 public exchange between Olavo de Carvalho and Aleksandr Dugin is worked through at length in the Curso Online de Filosofia, and what the lectures show is two readers disputing the same inheritance. Bishop José Luiz Ferreira Salles of Pesqueira signs, at Aldeia Guarda, a pastoral letter on the 'presumíveis aparições de Nossa Senhora da Graça': it states the reported events show 'elements, traces and signs that signal the great probability of the supernatural character' of the experience, finds the messages 'in deep consonance with the truths of the Christian faith', permits the devotion, and reserves interpretation of the events to the Church's magisterium and the diocesan bishop. Catholic press describes it as the Church's first official declaration on the case in 85 years. Read against the norms the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith would publish three years later, this letter already has the shape of their ordinary favourable outcome: it permits the devotion and speaks of the "great probability" of a supernatural character without declaring one. That is close to what art. 17 of the 2024 norms calls a "Nihil obstat", and it is why this rung records a judgment on the devotion rather than on the apparitions.

Medjugorje

The DDF Note 'The Queen of Peace': nihil obstat — and expressly not a supernaturality declaration

The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith publishes the Note 'The Queen of Peace', approved by Pope Francis in audience on 28 August 2024 and signed by Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández and Msgr. Armando Matteo. Under the 2024 Norms it grants a nihil obstat to the spiritual experience connected with Medjugorje: the faithful 'are authorized to give it their adherence in a prudent manner', public acts of devotion are authorized — 'While this does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon in question… and recalling that the faithful are not obliged to believe in it'. The messages must always be described as 'alleged messages'; 'some less important or unclear texts should be read with prudence'; future messages require the Apostolic Visitor's approval before publication, and the Note itself must preface any collection. At the presentation press conference the DDF quoted the shrine's own registers — 47.4 million Communions since 1985 — as the scale of the devotion being regulated. The most favourable Church act in Medjugorje's history, and its own text is the primary witness to what it does not decide.

The ninetieth anniversary of the first reported apparition is marked at the shrine and covered by the Pernambuco secular press. What has been built at the site over the intervening decades is modest and mostly by donation: a chapel below, a 365-step stairway with a handrail up to the rock where the reported figure was seen, retaining walls, shelter for pilgrims, and the Mirante do Cruzeiro viewpoint opened in 2005 at 744 metres. Mass is celebrated in the open beside the chapel; the annual feast falls on 31 August; Fr. Mailson Régis de Queirós CSsR took over as rector in January 2026.
