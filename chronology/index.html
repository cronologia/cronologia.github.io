Master chronology

Every event of the nine project chronologies on one timeline — filter by project, click any event for the full cited entry on its project site. Intersections the projects document separately become visible side by side here.

Before 1900

1540
Perennialism

Steuco coins 'philosophia perennis'

Agostino Steuco's 'De perenni philosophia' introduces the term — for a continuity thesis inside Christian Platonism, centuries before and apart from the Guénonian school.

1900s

1901
RCC

Protestant precursor: modern Pentecostalism

The Topeka outpouring (1901) and the Azusa Street revival (1906) launch modern Pentecostalism — context for, not part of, the Catholic movement.

1908
TFP

Birth of Plinio Corrêa de Oliveira

Born into a traditional paulista family; Jesuit schooling (Colégio São Luís), law at the future USP.

1910s

1912 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Guénon's Sufi affiliation

René Guénon adopts Islam in the Shadhili lineage transmitted through Ivan Aguéli (Abd al-Hadi); sources date the affiliation 1912 and the initiation itself variously c. 1910–1912.

1920s

1921
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'Le Théosophisme'

Guénon publishes his critical history of the Theosophical Society — the Traditionalist critique of modern 'counter-tradition' begins in print.

1921
Perennialism

Guénon's first books

'Introduction générale à l'étude des doctrines hindoues' frames 'Tradition' against modernity; 'Le Théosophisme' separates the school-to-be from occultism and Theosophy.

1923
Perennialism

'L'Erreur spirite'

Guénon's companion polemic against Spiritism completes the boundary-drawing against the occult milieu.

1924
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'Orient et Occident'

Guénon's book marks his break with the French Catholic intellectuals among whom he had first written.

1924
Perennialism

'Orient et Occident'

Guénon's program for a Western elite reoriented by Eastern metaphysics — and his break with the Catholic intellectuals among whom he had first written.

1925 ?
Perennialism

'L'Homme et son devenir selon le Vêdânta'

Guénon's central metaphysical exposition; the standard date is 1925, though a derived source gives 1927 — flagged until checked against a critical bibliography.

1925
Perennialism

The Regnabit episode

Guénon contributes nineteen articles (August 1925 – May 1927) to the Catholic Sacred Heart review Regnabit, drawn in by the symbolist scholar Louis Charbonneau-Lassay — the high point of his attempted rapprochement with Catholic milieux, ended by his withdrawal under Catholic criticism.

1927 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'La Crise du monde moderne'

Guénon publishes the school's most famous statement of civilizational critique.

1927
Perennialism

'La Crise du monde moderne'

The school's most-read statement of civilizational critique — the template for all its later 'crisis' literature.

1928
TFP

The Marian Congregations

Plinio joins São Paulo's Marian Congregations — the milieu from which his group would grow.

1929
Perennialism

'Autorité spirituelle et pouvoir temporel'

Guénon asserts the priority of the sacerdotal over the royal — the doctrinal fault line that would divide him from Evola.

1930s

1930
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Guénon moves to Cairo

He lives there as Abd al-Wahid Yahya until his death, marrying Fatima Ibrahim (1934) and taking Egyptian citizenship (1949).

1930
Perennialism

Guénon settles in Cairo

He writes thereafter as a Muslim while addressing a mainly French readership, until his death in 1951.

1932 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Schuon initiated in the Alawiyya

Frithjof Schuon enters Islam and is initiated at the zawiya of Shaykh Ahmad al-Alawi, receiving the name Isa Nur ad-Din; Wikipedia's own articles date the stay 1932 or 1933–34 — the discrepancy is recorded, not resolved.

1932
Perennialism

Coomaraswamy's metaphysical turn

The Boston Museum of Fine Arts curator shifts from art history to studies of tradition, myth and metaphysics — the essays of 1932–1947 make him the school's comparativist of 'traditional art', Vedanta and Platonism.

1933
TFP

Youngest constituent deputy

Co-founder of the Liga Eleitoral Católica, Plinio is elected to the National Constituent Assembly on São Paulo's Chapa Única at 24 — the youngest deputy of the Constituinte (sympathetic sources add 'most voted in the country'; attributed). The 1934 Constitution incorporated points of the Catholic platform.

1934
Perennialism

Evola's 'Revolt Against the Modern World'

Julius Evola's political-heroic adaptation of Traditionalism subordinates the priestly to the regal — against Guénon's 1929 position; Guénon's review rejected key theses while granting the work's interest.

1935 ?
TFP

Director of O Legionário

Plinio directs the archdiocesan weekly O Legionário — named director in August 1933 per movement sources, effective from about 1935 per Wikipedia; the divergence is recorded. He leaves the paper in 1947.

1935
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Schuon made muqaddam

Shaykh Adda ben Tounes, al-Alawi's successor, authorizes Schuon to initiate aspirants into the Alawi order in Europe.

1936
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Independent shaykh

Schuon assumes the function of shaykh of an independent branch. Followers' accounts describe a divine investiture confirmed by disciples' dreams; Sedgwick treats it as self-authorization — both framings are attributed.

1936
Perennialism

'Études Traditionnelles' is born

Le Voile d'Isis (founded 1889) is renamed Études Traditionnelles, reflecting Guénon's dominance of the review — the school's house journal until 1992, with a wartime gap 1940–45.

1939
Perennialism

'Peaks and Lamas'

Marco Pallis's best-selling mountaineering-and-Buddhism book carries a Traditionalist reading of Tibet to a wide audience.

1940s

1940
TFP

President of São Paulo's Catholic Action

First president of the Archdiocesan Junta of Catholic Action (to 1944).

1940
Perennialism

'Look to the Land' coins 'organic farming'

Lord Northbourne — later a Traditionalist author and translator of Guénon — introduces the term 'organic farming', framing agriculture as a clash of worldviews; the school's most unexpected export.

1941
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The order settles in Lausanne

Schuon directs the order from Lausanne for four decades (1941–1980).

1943
TFP

'Em Defesa da Ação Católica'

Plinio's polemic against 'progressive'/Maritainian currents in Catholic Action, prefaced by nuncio Aloisi Masella — a storm of controversy that begins his estrangement from the paulista curia.

1945
Perennialism

'The Reign of Quantity'

Guénon's summa on modernity as the reign of quantity is published in the Liberation moment.

1945
Perennialism

Huxley's other 'Perennial Philosophy'

Aldous Huxley's bestselling anthology popularizes the phrase in a universalist-mystical sense — indifferent to orthodoxy and initiation — that the Guénonians rejected as syncretism. Same words, different lineage.

1947
TFP

Break with the curia

Under the new archbishop Carlos Carmelo de Vasconcelos Motta, Plinio loses the Catholic Action presidency (1944) and the Legionário direction (1947) — ecclesial ostracism. Sympathetic sources highlight a laudatory 1949 letter signed by Msgr. Montini in Pius XII's name; the document is reproduced only by movement sites, so it is recorded as their claim.

1947
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Plains Indian dimension begins

After reading 'Black Elk Speaks', Schuon initiates contact with Lakota tradition-holders; he and his wife are later adopted into the Sioux family of Chief James Red Cloud (1959) and by Thomas Yellowtail into the Crow (1984).

1947
Perennialism

Death of Ananda Coomaraswamy

1948
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'The Transcendent Unity of Religions'

Schuon publishes his best-known statement of perennialism (English translation 1953).

1948
Perennialism

'The Transcendent Unity of Religions'

Schuon's classic statement of the transcendent unity of religions (English 1953); T. S. Eliot: 'I have met with no more impressive work in the comparative study of Oriental and Occidental religion.'

1950s

1950
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Vâlsan split

Per Sedgwick: disciples object to Schuon's relaxation of Islamic practice; Guénon encourages Michel Vâlsan to separate the Paris branch, and Vâlsan establishes an independent, sharia-observant Alawiyya line with Guénon's approval.

1951
TFP

Catolicismo founded

The monthly Catolicismo is founded under the patronage of Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer of Campos — the group's platform for the next decade.

1951
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Death of René Guénon

1951
Perennialism

Death of René Guénon

1951
Perennialism

Evola tried and acquitted

Arrested as alleged ideologue of the neofascist FAR, Evola declares himself 'superfascista' while denying party fascism, and is acquitted — the trial that fixes his postwar political persona.

1955 ?
Teologia da Libertação

CELAM founded

The Latin American Episcopal Council is created at the first General Conference of Latin American bishops.

1955
CELAM

I General Conference

Convoked by Pius XII, the first General Conference of the Latin American Episcopate petitions for a permanent coordinating body.

1955 ?
CELAM

Pius XII approves CELAM

The pope approves the creation of the Consejo Episcopal Latinoamericano; one source records a 24 September approval with the formal founding document on 2 November — the sequence is recorded, not resolved.

1956 ?
CELAM

Secretariat in Bogotá; first Assembly

Bogotá is designated as seat (Rome having been considered and rejected, per DHIAL); the first ordinary General Assembly elects Cardinal Jaime de Barros Câmara (Rio) first president.

1958
Perennialism

'Sacred Art in East and West'

Titus Burckhardt's canonical Traditionalist theory of sacred art across five traditions (English translation by Lord Northbourne).

1958
Perennialism

'The Religions of Man'

Huston Smith's textbook (later 'The World's Religions') dominates American comparative-religion teaching; his openly perennialist 'Forgotten Truth' (1976) follows under Schuon's influence.

1958
CELAM

CAL created

Pius XII creates the Pontifical Commission for Latin America in the Roman Curia — CELAM's standing liaison with the Holy See.

1959
CELAM

CLAR erected

The Holy See erects the Confederación Latinoamericana de Religiosos, CELAM's sister body for religious life.

1959
TFP

'Revolução e Contra-Revolução'

Published in Catolicismo's 100th issue: the movement's doctrinal matrix, reading modern history as a five-century 'Revolution' and calling for a 'Counter-Revolution' — Plinio's technical vocabulary, attributed to the text.

1960s

1960
TFP

TFP founded

The Sociedade Brasileira de Defesa da Tradição, Família e Propriedade is founded; co-founders include Adolpho Lindenberg, Plinio Vidigal Xavier da Silveira and José Fernando de Camargo — the lists vary slightly between sources.

1960
TFP

'Reforma Agrária: Questão de Consciência'

Four authors — Bishop Geraldo de Proença Sigaud, Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer, Plinio Corrêa de Oliveira and the economist Luiz Mendonça de Freitas — publish the anti-agrarian-reform manifesto; about 30,000 copies over four editions (movement figures, attributed).

1961
Perennialism

Evola's postwar canon

'Men Among the Ruins' (1953) and 'Ride the Tiger' (1961) join 'Revolt' as the canon of the postwar Italian radical right, of which scholarship describes Evola as chief ideologue.

1961 ?
Perennialism

'Understanding Islam'

Schuon's most popular book (French 1961, English 1963) becomes a key channel of perennialism into Islamic studies; the French first-edition year awaits confirmation against the standard bibliography.

1961 ?
Teologia da Libertação

ISAL founded

Iglesia y Sociedad en América Latina, the radicalizing Protestant 'church and society' network (Alves, Shaull, Míguez Bonino), a precursor stream; sources differ on the founding consultation's exact venue.

1962
Teologia da Libertação

Second Vatican Council opens

The Council (1962–1965) opens the space that Latin American bishops later 'receive' at Medellín.

1963
TFP

The Coetus Internationalis Patrum

Sigaud, Castro Mayer and Marcel Lefebvre articulate the conservative minority of Vatican II; movement sources describe Plinio as the group's lay strategist — that role rests on sympathetic sources and is attributed.

1965 ?
TFP

The street campaigns

Caravans under red standards with a golden lion, public rosaries and signature drives against communism and 'socialist and confiscatory' agrarian reform make the TFP a fixture of Brazilian streets.

1965
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Marian visions

Schuon reports visions of the Virgin Mary ('Marian graces') and thereafter regards Mary as the order's spiritual protectress — the origin of the order's distinctive Marian devotion.

1965 ?
CELAM

Expansion to nine departments

CELAM grows from two sub-departments to nine specialized departments (education, liturgy, seminaries, social action…).

1966
Teologia da Libertação

Death of Camilo Torres

The Colombian priest and sociologist, who had joined the ELN guerrilla, is killed in his first combat; later invoked as a precursor symbol, though his choice of armed struggle was not representative of liberation theologians.

1966
CELAM

Death of Manuel Larraín

The president dies in a car accident; the X Ordinary Assembly (Mar del Plata 1966) elects Avelar Brandão Vilela to complete the term.

1967
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Bloomington chapter founded

Indiana University professor Victor Danner founds the Bloomington chapter, which becomes the order's most important American center.

1967
RCC

The Word of God community founded

Ralph Martin, Stephen Clark, Jim Cavnar and Gerry Rauch found the Word of God — matrix of the 'covenant communities'.

1967
RCC

The Duquesne Weekend

About 25 students of Duquesne University's Chi Rho group, on retreat at The Ark and the Dove, report experiences of baptism in the Holy Spirit — the founding moment of the Catholic Charismatic Renewal. Some popular accounts say 17–18 February; 17–19 is the solid dating.

1967
RCC

The movement reaches Notre Dame

Two weeks after Duquesne, nine people — among them Kevin and Dorothy Ranaghan — pray for baptism in the Spirit at the University of Notre Dame; the first 'international' gathering follows there on 7–9 April, seed of the annual Notre Dame conferences that would draw tens of thousands in the 1970s.

1967
Teologia da Libertação

Populorum Progressio

Paul VI's encyclical on 'the development of peoples', a key reference for the movement.

1968
Teologia da Libertação

Gutiérrez's Chimbote lecture

Asked to speak on a 'theology of development', Gustavo Gutiérrez reframes the topic as 'Hacia una teología de la liberación'.

1968
TFP

The petition to Paul VI

The 'Message to Paul VI' against 'communist infiltration' in the Church gathers 1,600,368 signatures in Brazil (2,025,201 with the Southern Cone TFPs) — the movement's own numbers, always attributed.

1968
Teologia da Libertação

CELAM II (Medellín)

Paul VI opens the conference in Bogotá on 24 August (first papal visit to Latin America); working sessions in Medellín 26 August – 6 September adopt the language of 'institutionalized violence' and the option for the poor.

1968
CELAM

II General Conference (Medellín)

Paul VI opens the conference in Bogotá cathedral — the first papal visit to Latin America; the working sessions sit in Medellín, producing the Medellín Documents. The interpretation disputes belong to the tl project.

1969
Teologia da Libertação

Rubem Alves, 'A Theology of Human Hope'

The Brazilian Presbyterian's 1968 Princeton dissertation 'Towards a Theology of Liberation' is published (retitled by the publisher) — the Protestant pioneer text.

1969
Tariqa Maryamiyya

'Maryamiyya' enters the name

The order formally becomes the Tariqa Shadhiliyya-Darqawiyya-Alawiyya-Maryamiyya.

1969 ?
RCC

Arrival in Brazil (movement's account)

US Jesuit Fr. Haroldo Rahm begins 'Prayer Courses and Studies on the Holy Spirit' in Campinas in the second half of 1969 — RCC Brasil cites a 15–17 August 1969 experience with about 60 young people — and Brazil's first prayer group is born. The academic literature (Prandi) dates the movement's Brazilian take-off to the mid-1970s; both datings are recorded.

1970s

1970
TFP

Sigaud breaks with the TFP

Archbishop Sigaud breaks with Plinio and the movement, supporting the Médici government's agrarian program and diverging on liturgical-ecclesial questions.

1970
FSSPX

Founding of the SSPX

Bishop François Charrière erects the Society as a pia unio on an ad experimentum basis for six years; Marcel Lefebvre is founder and superior.

1971
FSSPX

Écône seminary opens

The International Seminary of Saint Pius X receives its first cohort of 24 candidates; 32 more enter in October 1972.

1971
Teologia da Libertação

Gutiérrez publishes 'Teología de la liberación'

The movement's foundational text; English translation 'A Theology of Liberation' follows in 1973 (Orbis).

1971
Teologia da Libertação

Casaldáliga becomes bishop

Pedro Casaldáliga begins his emblematic 'church of the poor' prelature in the Brazilian Amazon (served 1971–2005).

1972 ?
Teologia da Libertação

López Trujillo elected CELAM secretary-general

Alfonso López Trujillo begins the institutional pushback against liberation theology from within CELAM.

1972 ?
RCC

National diffusion begins

Rahm publishes 'Sereis Batizados no Espírito', presenting 'Catholic Pentecostalism'; Fr. Eduardo Dougherty SJ, ordained 1971, returns to Brazil and spreads retreats and prayer groups across the country (movement's account).

1972
Teologia da Libertação

Christians for Socialism encounter

First Latin American Encounter of Cristianos por el Socialismo (~400 delegates); the movement had begun in April 1971 when some 80 Chilean priests declared support for socialism.

1972
CELAM

The Sucre Assembly

The XIV Ordinary Assembly restructures the departments, self-defines CELAM as an 'organism of communion and service', and elects Alfonso López Trujillo secretary general (to 1979) — widely read by scholars as an institutional turning point; that reading is attributed.

1973 ?
TFP

The international network

Sister TFPs form in Argentina, Chile and Uruguay (1967), then Spain (1971), the United States (incorporated 18 May 1973 as the Foundation for a Christian Civilization — PT Wikipedia says 1974; the divergence is recorded), Canada (1975), France (1977), Germany (1982) and beyond.

1973 ?
RCC

First National Congress of the RCC

About 50 leaders meet, organized by Dougherty, Rahm and Sr. Juliette Schuckenbrock; a second congress follows in Belo Horizonte in 1974 (movement's account).

1973 ?
Teologia da Libertação

Chilean bishops ban Christians for Socialism

After the Pinochet coup, the Chilean episcopate forbids clergy membership in the movement.

1974
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Huston Smith joins

The scholar of world religions joins the order after meeting Schuon, attending his first dhikr in Tehran — with Nasr, one of the two most famous academic followers per Sedgwick.

1974
CELAM

ITEPAL opens

The four regional formation institutes — including IPLA (Quito), absorbed per the Sucre restructuring — are merged into ITEPAL, which moves to Bogotá in 1989.

1974
RCC

The first Malines Document

Cardinal Léon-Joseph Suenens convenes theologians (drafting by Kilian McDonnell) and publishes 'Malines Document 1' — the theological-pastoral orientations that gave the movement doctrinal respectability.

1974
FSSPX

Canonical visitation

Two Belgian priests inspect the seminary on behalf of a commission of cardinals.

1974 ?
FSSPX

Lefebvre's Declaration

Lefebvre professes adherence to 'eternal Rome' while rejecting 'the Rome of neo-modernist tendencies' — the text that became the Society's ideological charter.

1975 ?
Perennialism

'Logic and Transcendence'

Schuon's fullest engagement with modern philosophy (French 1970, English 1975); French first-edition year flagged pending the standard bibliography.

1975
FSSPX

Approval withdrawn

Bishop Pierre Mamie withdraws the Society's pia unio status with the approval of the Congregation for Religious and Paul VI; the SSPX disputes the legality of the procedure.

1975
RCC

Paul VI: 'a chance for the Church'

At the movement's third international congress in the Holy Year — with a Pentecost Mass in St Peter's on 18 May — Paul VI calls the Renewal 'a chance for the Church and for the world'.

1976
FSSPX

The 'hot summer' ordinations

Lefebvre ordains priests despite an explicit prohibition, after being publicly rebuked by Paul VI in consistory on 24 May.

1976
FSSPX

Suspension a divinis

After a 1 July suspension from conferring orders and continued refusal to comply, Lefebvre is suspended from all sacramental ministry.

1978
RCC

Canção Nova founded

Fr. (later Msgr.) Jonas Abib founds the Canção Nova community — the future media powerhouse of the Brazilian Renewal.

1978 ?
FSSPX

Lefebvre meets John Paul II

Shortly after the papal election, Lefebvre seeks regularization; talks continue intermittently through the 1980s.

1979
Teologia da Libertação

CELAM III (Puebla)

John Paul II's opening address (28 January) warns against 're-readings' of Christ as political revolutionary while affirming the defense of human dignity; the conference document consolidates the 'preferential option for the poor'.

1979
CELAM

III General Conference (Puebla)

Opened by John Paul II on 28 January; concluding document on evangelization in Latin America's present and future.

1980s

1980
FSSPX

Latin American seminary founded

The Seminario Nuestra Señora Corredentora is founded in the Buenos Aires priory and moves to La Reja in 1981 — the formation house for Latin America, including Brazil.

1980
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Schuon moves to Bloomington

Schuon and his wife emigrate from Lausanne to Bloomington, where a large community of disciples already lived; the publishing house World Wisdom is founded there the same year.

1980
Perennialism

'Temenos' launches

Kathleen Raine, Keith Critchlow, Brian Keeble and Philip Sherrard found the review devoted to 'the arts of the imagination' — seed of the Temenos Academy (1990), of which the Prince of Wales (now King Charles III) has been patron since its founding.

1980
RCC

Associação do Senhor Jesus

Fr. Eduardo Dougherty founds the Associação do Senhor Jesus: publications, music and the TV program 'Anunciamos o Senhor'. The same year Raniero Cantalamessa OFMCap, a preacher close to the Renewal, becomes Preacher of the Papal Household (year per CHARIS's biography of him).

1980
Teologia da Libertação

Assassination of Óscar Romero

The Archbishop of San Salvador is shot dead while celebrating Mass in the Divina Providencia hospital chapel.

1981
Perennialism

Nasr's Gifford Lectures

Seyyed Hossein Nasr delivers the Gifford Lectures, published as 'Knowledge and the Sacred' — per the Gifford records, the first Muslim and first non-Westerner to do so: the school's academic high-water mark.

1981
RCC

John Paul II receives the leaders' conference

The pope addresses the fourth international leaders' conference of the Renewal — the first of his repeated receptions.

1982
FSSPX

Schmidberger succeeds Lefebvre

Fr. Franz Schmidberger becomes Superior General (1982–1994); Lefebvre remains the Society's episcopal head.

1982
RCC

Comunidade Católica Shalom founded

Moysés Louro de Azevedo Filho opens the 'evangelizing snack bar' in Fortaleza, with Cardinal Lorscheider present — birth of the Shalom community.

1982
RCC

Sword of the Spirit federation

A federation of covenant communities grows out of the Word of God (unified association 1982, international entity 1983; 82 communities, 45 Catholic, by 2017). The Word of God itself withdrew in 1990 amid documented controversies over authoritarian governance — an episode critics of covenant communities cite.

1983
FSSPX

The 'Nine' leave — SSPV founded

Fr. Clarence Kelly and eight US priests are expelled from or leave the SSPX in a dispute over the 1962 Missal and openness to sedevacantism, founding the Society of Saint Pius V — a distinct, sedevacantist-leaning body.

1983
Perennialism

Lings's 'Muhammad'

Martin Lings's prize-winning biography becomes the school's biggest crossover into mainstream Islamic readership.

1983 ?
Teologia da Libertação

John Paul II reprimands Ernesto Cardenal

The pope publicly admonishes the priest-poet, then Sandinista culture minister, on the Managua airport tarmac — the famous wagging-finger image.

1984
Teologia da Libertação

Cardenal suspended a divinis

Ernesto Cardenal is suspended from priestly ministry under John Paul II for holding government office.

1984
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Nasr's Washington platform

Seyyed Hossein Nasr becomes University Professor of Islamic Studies at George Washington University and founds the Foundation for Traditional Studies, publisher of the journal Sophia.

1984
TFP

The Venezuelan crisis

A press campaign and parliamentary inquiry target the 'Resistencia-TFP', with a member arrested on suspicion of a plot against John Paul II and accusations of being a 'sect'; the accusations never produced a conviction in the sources consulted, the Venezuelan TFP was effectively extinguished by around 1986, and every characterization here is attributed.

1984
Teologia da Libertação

CDF Instruction 'Libertatis Nuntius'

The Instruction on Certain Aspects of the 'Theology of Liberation', signed by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, warns against the uncritical borrowing of Marxist analysis while not condemning the theme of liberation itself.

1985
Teologia da Libertação

Notification on Leonardo Boff

The CDF issues a Notification on Boff's 'Church: Charism and Power'; Boff is subsequently subjected to a period of imposed silence, lifted in 1986.

1985
TFP

The CNBB note

A CNBB commission (Bishops Gilberto Pereira Lopes, Antonio Misiara, Carlos Alberto Navarro) declares 'notorious the lack of communion of the TFP with the Church in Brazil', citing 'esoteric character, religious fanaticism, cult of the leader's personality and abusive use of the name of Mary', and exhorts Catholics not to join. The full text circulates mainly via the TFP's own replies — the CNBB original is a documented gap.

1986 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

The Brazilian branch founded

Olavo de Carvalho leads a group of Brazilians to Bloomington, is initiated (taking the name Sidi Muhammad Ibrahim, per the critical documentary blog, corroborated in outline by Sedgwick), and is appointed muqaddam in Brazil under Martin Lings's guidance — described as the first tariqa brought to Brazil by Brazilians. Sources split between 1986 (Sedgwick) and 1987 (Brazilian tertiary sources) for the founding.

1986
Teologia da Libertação

CDF Instruction 'Libertatis Conscientia'

The Instruction on Christian Freedom and Liberation gives a more positive treatment of liberation themes.

1986 ?
Teologia da Libertação

John Paul II's letter to the Brazilian bishops

The pope writes that liberation theology, rightly understood, is 'not only opportune but useful and necessary' — a passage cited by the movement's defenders.

1987
FSSPX

Consecrations announced

Lefebvre announces his intention to consecrate bishops without a papal mandate, citing his age and the need to continue ordinations.

1988
FSSPX

Protocol signed — and withdrawn

Lefebvre and Cardinal Ratzinger sign a protocol of agreement; Lefebvre withdraws his assent the following day.

1988
FSSPX

The Écône consecrations

Lefebvre, with Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer (emeritus of Campos, Brazil) as co-consecrator, consecrates Bernard Fellay, Bernard Tissier de Mallerais, Richard Williamson and Alfonso de Galarreta without papal mandate.

1988
TFP

Castro Mayer at Écône — without the TFP

Castro Mayer co-consecrates Lefebvre's four bishops and incurs the declared excommunication (Ecclesia Dei). The TFP did not accompany him and remained formally in communion — the key TFP ≠ SSPX distinction; Castro Mayer's Campos succession is documented in the fsspx project.

1988
FSSPX

Excommunications declared

The Congregation for Bishops declares that Lefebvre, Castro Mayer and the four new bishops incurred latae sententiae excommunication.

1988
FSSPX

'Ecclesia Dei'

John Paul II's motu proprio calls the consecrations 'a schismatic act' and establishes the Ecclesia Dei Commission for faithful attached to the traditional liturgy.

1988
FSSPX

FSSP founded

Twelve former SSPX priests led by Fr. Josef Bisig found the Priestly Fraternity of Saint Peter in full communion with Rome.

1988
FSSPX

Campos priests form the Priestly Union of Saint John Mary Vianney

The priests loyal to Bishop Castro Mayer formally organize the União Sacerdotal São João Maria Vianney, continuing the traditional Mass across the diocese of Campos.

1989 ?
Tariqa Maryamiyya

Olavo breaks with Schuon

Olavo de Carvalho leaves the order after a rupture with Schuon; the sources disagree on the date — 1987 (Bunker da Cultura), 'the late 1980s' (Sedgwick), or an expelled-1987–91 window (the Jardim series) — and on the reasons, with his own published framing (the multiconfessional rule, in 'Influências discretas') differing from the hostile accounts; every version is attributed. He reinvents himself, in Sedgwick's phrase, as a Catholic-identifying 'post-Traditionalist'.

1989
Teologia da Libertação

UCA massacre

A Salvadoran army unit murders six Jesuits — including rector and philosopher Ignacio Ellacuría — and Elba and Celina Ramos at the Central American University.

1990s

1990
Foro de São Paulo

I Encontro — São Paulo, Brazil

Founding meeting. 48 parties/organizations from 14 countries.

1990
RCC

TV Século 21 and Obra de Maria

Dougherty's association launches the satellite channel TV Século 21 (today Rede Século 21); in Recife, Gilberto Gomes Barbosa founds the Obra de Maria community, focused on pilgrimages and missions.
